## Chunk 001 (pages 1-9, sections: Abstract, Introduction, Section 2 CRC model and extrapolation through 2.4)

### Narrative notes
- CRC models accommodate heterogeneous treatment effects and flexible selection; in simplest form yield diff-in-diff for movers. They do NOT identify ATE for stayers (those with constant treatment status over time).
- Paper derives additional restrictions that permit extrapolation from identified mover quantities to stayer ATE. Develops both a "simple/non-robust" extrapolation (nests Lemieux 1998 and Suri 2011) and a new "robust extrapolation" that allows observed correlated cost shifters.
- Model setup: $y_{it}(0) = a_i + f_t + u_{0,it}$, $y_{it}(1) = y_{it}(0) + b_i + u_{1,it}$, with strict exogeneity $E(u_{d,it}|X) = 0$; $b_i$ is time-constant treatment effect; CRC model (2.4) combines to $y_{it} = a_i + f_t + b_i x_{it} + u_{it}$.
- For T=2, identification: time-effect differences recovered from stayers ($E(\Delta y_i | 0,0) = \Delta f$); ATE for movers via diff-in-diff; ATE for stayers not restricted by model.
- Extrapolation identifying assumption (2.11) = linear projection of $b_i$ on $a_i$ is the SAME for stayers and movers. Sufficient conditions: (i) linear conditional mean $E(a_i|b_i) = \alpha_0 + \alpha_1 b_i$, (ii) factors of selection other than $b_i$ (cost shifters $c_{it}$) are independent of $(a_i, b_i)$.
- Extrapolation has simple graphical representation: line through mover points $(E(a_i|1,0), E(b_i|1,0))$ and $(E(a_i|0,1), E(b_i|0,1))$ is fit, then interpolated with vertical $a = E(a_i|0,0)$ for untreated stayers and -45 degree line for treated stayers.
- Paper notes Suri (2011) asserts assumption (2.11) is "not restrictive" (p. 181) but Verdier argues it is restrictive when cost shifters correlate with baseline heterogeneity/treatment effect, which is common.

## Chunk 002 (pages 11-21, sections: 2.5 Correlated cost shifters, 2.6 Additional covariates, 2.7 Time-varying effects, 3.1 First-step CRC regression, 3.2 Non-robust extrapolation, 3.3 Robust extrapolation)

### Narrative notes
- Section 2.5: generalizes extrapolation when cost of treatment $c_{it} = g(w_i) + n_{it}$, with observed $w_i$. Generalized assumption (2.18) conditions on $w_i$; stayer ATE identified as $E(b_i|0,0) = E(a_i - m(w_i)|0,0)/a_1$ and analogously for treated stayers.
- Section 2.6: CRC model with control covariates $z_{it}$; $g_0$ identified from within-time variation in $z_{it}$; extrapolation proceeds after partialling out $z_{it} g_0$. Paper notes Lemieux/Suri stronger assumption (2.23) imposes no partial predictive effect of BOTH $x$-history AND $z$-history on $a_i$; Verdier's (2.18) only needs $x$-history restriction.
- Section 2.7: CRC with time-varying treatment effects = semiparametric DID of Abadie (2005). Can re-cast as CRC with larger number of random coefficients ($T$ coefficients per $i$).
- Estimation procedure is two-step. Step 1: OLS of $y_{it}$ on individual dummies, individual-dummy-x-treatment interactions, and time dummies. Yields $\hat{f}_t$ precisely; yields noisy $\hat{a}_i, \hat{b}_i$ for movers; only $\hat{a}_i$ for untreated stayers and only $\hat{a}_i + \hat{b}_i$ for treated stayers. Proposition 1 shows noise in heterogeneity estimates decomposes into $O_p(1/\sqrt{n})$ part plus non-vanishing conditional-mean-zero noise.
- Step 2a (non-robust extrapolation, section 3.2): IV regression of $\hat{a}_i$ on $\hat{b}_i$ using treatment-history $\{x_{i1},\ldots,x_{iT}\}$ as instruments, on movers only. With T=2 this is a Wald estimator. Parameters: $a_0$ (intercept) and $a_1$ (slope of extrapolation line). Plug-in stayer ATE: $\widehat{ATE}_{00} = (\bar{a}_{00} - \hat{a}_0)/\hat{a}_1$ and $\widehat{ATE}_{11} = (\overline{a+b}_{11} - \hat{a}_0)/(1+\hat{a}_1)$.
- Step 2b (robust extrapolation, section 3.3): fixed-effects IV regression of $\hat{a}_i$ on $\hat{b}_i$ using treatment history as instruments, with village/cluster fixed effects indexed by $v_i$. Recovers $\hat{a}_1$ and cluster fixed effects $\hat{e}_v$. Stayer ATE plug-in uses $\hat{e}_v$ in place of $\hat{a}_0$. Cluster bootstrap for inference, clusters = $v_i$.
- Assumption 3.b is the RELEVANCE condition: $E(b_i|0,1) \ne E(b_i|1,0)$ - the two mover groups must have different ATE. Assumption 3.a: both mover types have positive probability. $a_1 \notin \{0, -1\}$ needed for untreated/treated stayer ATE respectively.

## Chunk 003 (pages 22-29, sections: 3.4 Testing extrapolation validity, 4 Empirical application through 4.3.2)

### Narrative notes
- With T=2, extrapolation assumption (2.11) has zero testable implications: it defines four previously-free parameters as identities in CRC-identified quantities. With T>=3, more than two mover groups give over-identifying restrictions. Test = overidentification test after IV regression of $\hat{a}_i$ on $\hat{b}_i$ using $x_{i1},\ldots,x_{iT}$ as instruments. Analogous overidentification test for robust extrapolation following FE-IV.
- Empirical application: 1,130 Kenyan maize farmers, 3,770 observations (unbalanced), waves 1997/2004/2007/2010. 354 movers, 123 untreated stayers (never-hybrid), 653 treated stayers (always-hybrid). Adoption rose 72% (1997) -> 88% (2010); distance to nearest seller fell from 6.1km to 2.6-3.3km.
- CRC model specification: $y_{it}$ = log maize yield (kg/acre), $x_{it}$ = hybrid seed use, $z_{it}$ = same controls as Suri (2011) plus province-by-year FE. $a_i$ = baseline productivity; $b_i$ = returns to hybrid seeds.
- CRC overidentification test (testing strict exogeneity + parallel trends + time-constant TE): $E(D e_{it} | x_{i1},\ldots,x_{iT}) = 0$ after partialling $z_{it} g_t$. In this app, grouped form with $m_i$ = stayer indicator. p-value = 32% - does NOT reject CRC.
- Mover-ATE heterogeneity: early-adopters-who-disadopted have low returns (8-11%); late adopters have high returns (23-28%). Movers not-using-hybrid in 1997 have 37% returns; not-using in 2010 have ~0%. Overall mover ATE ~23%.
- Simple extrapolation results: $\hat{a}_1 = -0.49$ (negative slope - low-productivity farmers have higher returns). Plug-in stayer ATE = 66% for non-hybrid stayers. Suri (2011) got similar slope (-0.5 to -0.66) and even larger stayer ATE (100%).
- Evidence simple extrapolation fails: (a) adding $\bar{d}_i$ = average distance to nearest seed seller as exogenous covariate yields $\hat{a}_2 = -0.04$ with p=2.8%, (b) direct overidentification test has p<1%. Intuition: if distance (cost shifter) has partial predictive effect on $a_i$, treatment history predicts $a_i$ through both $b_i$ and $c_{it}$ channels, violating (2.11).
- Robust extrapolation: village-FE IV regression. $v_i$ = village. $\hat{a}_1 = -0.95$ (closer to -1 when controlling for village-level cost shifters). Stayer ATE for untreated = 37% (much smaller than 66%). 91% of non-hybrid stayers live in a village with at least one mover (overlap requirement). Overidentification test p=0.26 - does NOT reject robust extrapolation. Since $\hat{a}_1 \approx -1$, treated-stayer ATE not reported.
- Estimation is GMM (optimally weighted), which gives efficiency gain over 2SLS. Misspecification gives visible divergence between 2SLS and GMM lines.
