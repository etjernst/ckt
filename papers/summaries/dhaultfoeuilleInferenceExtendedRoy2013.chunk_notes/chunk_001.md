## Narrative notes

- The paper extends the Roy (1951) sectoral choice model along two dimensions: it adds a non-pecuniary component $G(X)$ to the selection equation and allows agents to act on expected rather than realized potential earnings, with the aim of disentangling monetary incentives from preferences in sorting decisions.
- The headline identification claim is that the non-pecuniary component is point identified once the covariate effects on earnings are known, provided at least one covariate is continuous; the authors emphasize this requires neither exclusion restrictions nor large-support conditions, contrasting with generalized Roy models (Heckman and Vytlacil, 2007; French and Taber, 2011).
- Section 2.2 develops the core identification argument: differentiating $E[D\eta_\Delta|X]$ and $q_0(x)=E[D|X=x]$ with respect to the continuous covariate $x_1$ links $G(x)$ to the observable derivative $\partial g_0/\partial x_1$ via Eq. (2.6), where $g_0(x)=E(\varepsilon|X=x)$. When $X$ is fully discrete, the same logic yields bounds via finite differences instead of point identification.
- Section 2.3 argues that the distribution of the ex ante return $\Delta$ is point or partially identified through the link between $\eta_\Delta$ in the selection equation and $\Delta$; large support of $T(X)+G(X)$ delivers full identification, otherwise the authors derive bounds (Eqs. 2.7-2.8) and an analogous expression for the treatment-on-the-treated distribution (Eq. 2.9). They also connect $\Delta$ to the marginal treatment effect of Heckman and Vytlacil (2005).
- Section 2.4 proposes two routes for identifying $(\psi_0,\psi_1)$: a Das et al. (2003)-style exclusion-restriction approach (Proposition 2.2) requiring measurable separability, and a non-standard identification-at-infinity argument (Proposition 2.3) leveraging right-tail behavior of outcomes that does not require exclusion restrictions or large support on $X$.
- The empirical application (previewed in the introduction) studies higher education attendance in France during the 1990s, using local average incomes for high-school and higher-education graduates as sector-specific regressors; the estimation section, however, restricts to the exclusion-restriction case since estimators for the identification-at-infinity strategy have not yet been derived.
