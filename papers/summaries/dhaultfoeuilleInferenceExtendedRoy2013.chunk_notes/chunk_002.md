## Narrative notes

- The paper sets up a semiparametric extended Roy model with linear indices for potential outcomes and selection, normalizing the index functions at zero, and lays out exclusion restriction, regularity, and i.i.d. sampling assumptions to support identification and estimation.
- A three-stage estimation procedure is proposed: a single-index estimator for the selection coefficient, series estimators for the outcome coefficients, and an IV-GMM step (with nonparametric kernel estimation of the propensity function) to recover the non-pecuniary intercept and slope parameters; an auxiliary theorem establishes root-n consistency and asymptotic normality.
- The third stage uses an instrumented linear model where the regressors involve the index U and an integral of the conditional choice probability, with trimming applied to manage boundary effects; exclusion restrictions on the non-pecuniary component can sharpen accuracy.
- The application studies the decision to attend higher education in France, using pooled Generation 1992 and 1998 surveys, with sector-specific local labor market variables (average log-earnings of high school vs higher education graduates by departement) serving as exclusion restrictions.
- Results report non-pecuniary determinants in unconstrained and constrained specifications (Table 1), and ex ante return distributions for the whole population and for higher education attendees (Fig. 1), highlighting that for most individuals the non-pecuniary component is negative ("psychic gain" of attending) and dispersion in ex ante returns is large.
- Robustness checks drop higher education dropouts and add a productivity proxy at the departement level; the authors also compute an analytical misspecification bias under generalized Roy assumptions with normally distributed unobservables.
