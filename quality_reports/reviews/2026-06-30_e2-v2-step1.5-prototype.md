## Verdict: qualified go

The estimand passes all three literal coherence tests at the CHN rural-hukou point estimates: where it can be calibrated, the realized effect and the unrealized potential are finite, sign-sensible, and stable, and the regime-convergence case does not blow up near the $\phi=-1$ pole.
But the $\sigma_\eta$ anchor to the observed never-migrant share is infeasible across most of $c\in[0,1]$: the model can reproduce the observed share only in a narrow corner near $c=1$ under a large-wedge normalization.
That breaks the spec's primary reporting object as written (M11's "full $c\in[0,1]$ range") and contradicts M11's premise that "$c$ has no data discipline."
So do not start the full build on the current M4/M11 framing.
Resolve the barrier-versus-never-share tension first (three options below); then the harness is worth building, because the underlying integral is well-behaved.

## What the prototype does

It reads only the already-exported per-cell input CSVs (`counterfactual_inputs/`), never the panel data junction.
At the point estimates it anchors the logistic shock scale to a never-migrant target under Reading A (barrier $\kappa$ in the baseline index), then evaluates both scenarios at $c\in\{0,0.5,1\}$ and computes the realized-effect integral $\sum_d \pi_d \int \Delta(\theta)[P^{cf}_d-P^{base}_d]\,dG_d$ and the unrealized potential $\sum_d \pi_d \int (1-P^{cf}_d)\max(\Delta,0)\,dG_d$.
The choice probability is logistic, $P=\Lambda((\Delta_{\text{payoff}}-\kappa)/s)$, with $\sigma_\eta = s\pi/\sqrt 3$, and the never probability over the balanced panel is $(1-P)^{T}$ with $T=4$.

Point inputs, read straight from the exports:
$\beta^{rh}=0.1046$, $\phi^{rh}=-0.0389$ (base trajectory 2, $\mu_{\text{base}}^{rh}=10.181$); $\beta^{uh}=0.1888$, $\phi^{uh}=-0.9731$ (base trajectory 4, $\mu_{\text{base}}^{uh}=10.328$).

## Sample and the person-level shares

The export collapses to one row per `pid` before counting (`keep if pid_first==1`), so `n_pids`, `dbar_d`, and `mu_d` in the trajectory CSVs are genuinely person-level, not row-level.
That lets the prototype anchor without touching the panel.
Among the 10,602 balanced rural-hukou persons the shares are never-migrant 0.6543, switcher 0.1010, always-urban 0.2447.
The prototype works in this balanced set, drops the unbalanced `-1` lump (58% of all rural-hukou persons, see blocker B2), and carries the always-urban mass only as a known-urban group that contributes $\pi$ to the denominator, zero to the never count, and zero to resorting (it has no exported rural $\mu_d$).

## Finding 1: the anchor is infeasible across most of the c range

Under Reading A the never share is $\sum_d \pi_d\,E_{G_d}[(1-P^{base})^{T}]$ with $P^{base}$ carrying the barrier $\kappa=c(\tilde\beta^{uh}-\tilde\beta^{rh})$.
As $s\to0$ the choice becomes a step rule and the never share goes to the mass of types with $\Delta^{rh}(\mu)<\kappa$; as $s\to\infty$ it goes to $0.5^{4}\times(1-\pi_{d_T})\approx0.047$.
The urban payoff is positive ($\Delta^{rh}\approx0.10$ almost flat in $\mu$), so unless $\kappa$ exceeds roughly $0.10$ the entire attainable range sits far below the observed 0.6543, and no $\sigma_\eta$ can reach it.

This is the central result, so here is the attainable range by normalization and $c$ (headline $\sigma_\theta=0.4$, target 0.6543):

| $\mu_{\text{common}}$ | wedge | $c=0$ | $c=0.5$ | $c=1$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pooled never mean | 0.2033 | infeasible [0, 0.047] | infeasible [0.047, 0.369] | feasible, $\sigma_\eta=0.052$ |
| rh base | 0.2274 | infeasible [0, 0.047] | infeasible [0.047, 0.516] | feasible, $\sigma_\eta=0.066$ |
| uh base | 0.0899 | infeasible [0, 0.047] | infeasible [0.0002, 0.047] | infeasible [0.047, 0.123] |

The data therefore discipline $c$ hard: only $c$ near 1, and only under a large-wedge normalization, can reproduce the baseline never share.
This directly contradicts M11's reasoning that $\sigma_\eta$ and $\kappa$ jointly fit the never share for any $c$; $\sigma_\eta$ cannot compensate for a too-small $\kappa$, because spreading the shock can only pull the never share toward 0.047, never up toward 0.65.
The economics behind it is the paper's own story: rural-hukou never-migrants stay rural despite a $+10\%$ urban return, so the barrier that rationalizes them is large (about $0.19$ log points by the back-of-envelope $\Lambda^{-1}(0.10)\approx-2.18$), larger than the return-schedule wedge under most normalizations.

## Finding 2: the wedge swings 2.5x with the normalization choice

$\tilde\beta^{uh}-\tilde\beta^{rh}$ is 0.090 at the urban-hukou base, 0.203 at the pooled never mean, and 0.227 at the rural-hukou base.
The swing is large because $\phi^{uh}=-0.97$ is steep, so $\tilde\beta^{uh}$ moves a lot as the reference type moves.
The plan asked us to "check the wedge is not sensitive to a reasonable alternative"; it is very sensitive.
Since $\kappa=c\times\text{wedge}$ sets the barrier, and feasibility (Finding 1) needs a large wedge, the whole calibration ends up pinned by a normalization choice rather than by an estimate.

## Finding 3: where feasible, the estimand is finite, sign-sensible, and stable

At $c=1$, $\mu_{\text{common}}$ = rh base, target 0.6543, logit, $T=4$:

- barrier-only: $\sigma_\eta=0.066$, realized $+0.072$ log points, unrealized $+0.004$.
- regime-convergence: $\sigma_\eta=0.066$, realized $+0.27$ log points, unrealized $+0.001$.

Both are positive and finite.
Removing the barrier moves the workers who benefit, so most of the potential is realized rather than left on the table, which is why unrealized is small next to realized in both arms.
Regime-convergence is larger because rural-hukou workers adopt the steep pro-poor urban-hukou schedule: at $\phi^{uh}=-0.97$ the low-$\mu$ types get very large urban returns (about $+0.70$ at $\mu=9.8$), so the resorting selects the low-$\theta$ tail.

The barrier-only headline is insensitive to $\sigma_\theta$ (realized $0.0719$ across $\sigma_\theta\in[0,0.6]$), because $\phi^{rh}\approx0$ makes the payoff nearly flat in type.
Regime-convergence is not: realized runs $0.245\to0.246\to0.271\to0.313\to0.356$ as $\sigma_\theta$ goes $0\to0.2\to0.4\to0.6\to0.8$, a 45% swing.
So the spec's worry that the $G_d$ tail drives the magnitude is real, but only in the regime-convergence arm; the full build must sweep $\sigma_\theta$ there and report the family, not just the dispersion (M5b).

## Finding 4: the phi = -1 pole does not bite the resorting object

Regime-convergence runs the rural-hukou workers on $\phi^{uh}=-0.9731$, just above $-1$, which was the standing worry (review Y1).
At the point estimate the realized and unrealized effects are finite at every feasible $c$ (e.g. $+0.27$ and $+0.001$ at $c=1$).
The reason is structural: the resorting integral evaluates the urban-hukou schedule at the workers' observed rural $\mu_d$, so it never divides by $1+\phi$.
The Mobius pole enters only through `lca_delta_dT`, the always-urban inversion, which the resorting object does not call (the always-urban group does not resort).
The full build still needs the stated $\epsilon$-exclusion around $\phi=-1$ for the envelope's accepted lattice (Step 5), but the point-estimate object itself is pole-free.

## Blockers for the full build

- B1 ($\sigma_\theta$). The within-trajectory comparative-advantage dispersion (calibration 1: dispersion of individual rural log consumption; calibration 2: the $\tau/\varepsilon$-stripped component) needs the panel, which this prototype does not read.
  I swept it instead. The full build needs a Stata export of the within-trajectory dispersion by cell, or panel access, before the regime-convergence magnitude can be pinned (Finding 3).
- B2 (unbalanced never share). The full rural-hukou person-level never share folds in the unbalanced `-1` lump (14,889 persons, 58% of the subsample), whose realized never-versus-switch status is not split in the current export.
  The prototype anchors to the balanced never share (0.6543) instead, which the CSV gives cleanly. The full build needs the unbalanced workers' per-person realized choice counts (or the split never/switcher counts within `-1`) exported from Stata, plus the empirical $T_i$ distribution (M6, M15, plan review G2).
- Neither blocker changes the verdict: Finding 1 (infeasibility for $c<1$) is robust to both, since it turns on $\kappa$ versus the positive urban payoff, not on $\sigma_\theta$ or the exact target.

## Recommended next step

Take the barrier-versus-never-share tension to the coauthors before building, with three options:

1. Free $\kappa$. Drop the $\kappa=c\,\text{wedge}$ cap; anchor $\kappa$ (or $\kappa$ jointly with $\sigma_\eta$ using a second moment) to the never share, then report the implied $\kappa\approx0.19$ next to the wedge as the diagnostic. This restores a feasible calibration and turns the wedge into a comparison object rather than the barrier itself.
2. Keep M4 but report honestly that only $c\approx1$ is feasible, so the "$c$ range" collapses to a point and $c$ is in fact disciplined by the data. This contradicts M11 and should be stated as such.
3. Reconsider Reading A. If the barrier is not in the baseline index, the never share is fit by $\sigma_\eta$ alone and the feasibility wall in Finding 1 moves; worth checking whether Reading B sidesteps it.

My recommendation is option 1: it is the cleanest fix, it keeps a feasible anchor across the whole $c$ range, and it surfaces the wedge's normalization sensitivity (Finding 2) as a reported diagnostic rather than burying it inside the barrier definition.
