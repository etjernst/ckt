### Verdier comparison

User asked how Verdier (2020) handles unbalanced panels. Read Appendix G of the Verdier online appendix (`papers/extracted/verdierAverageTreatmentEffects2020appendix`). Verdier's approach differs from CKT's in three ways.

1. Verdier assumes **unconditional** MAR ($o_{it} \perp$ everything); CKT uses conditional MAR.
2. Verdier does not partition individuals into balanced/unbalanced strata; he redefines stacked vectors $(Y_i, W_i, Z_i, X_i)$ over observed periods and pools everyone together. No $U_i$ indicator, no separate return parameter for unbalanced observers.
3. Verdier's step-1 uses cross-section dummies + cross-section-dummies-$\times$-treatment, i.e., individual fixed effects. CKT uses group-level trajectory intercepts $\mu_{\underline d}$.

So CKT's "common-$\gamma$ across strata" assumption is the cost of using group-level rather than individual-level intercepts; Verdier does not need it because his fixed-effects identification of $\gamma$ does not create strata. Parked a to-do note at `docs/reviews/2026-04-23-todo-verdier-section-G.md`.

### Common-$\gamma$ logic (user clarification thread)

User asked whether the common-$\gamma$ assumption also applies across trajectories, not just across strata. Reasoned through: yes, it does, but it is a direct consequence of A4 ($\gamma^U = \gamma^R$) in the main model. A4 says covariates affect consumption symmetrically across locations; since a trajectory is just a sequence of locations, A4 implies common covariate effects within and across trajectory cells. Under MAR, the implication extends to unbalanced observers, so Assumption~\ref{ass:common-gamma} in the proposition is formally a consequence of A4 + MAR, not new structure. Updated the proposition to state this explicitly.

### Trajectory-dummy analysis (docs/reviews/2026-04-23-unbalanced-trajectory-dummies.md)

User asked whether we could just add trajectory dummies for unbalanced observers instead of the single pooled cell, and how many new parameters that would require. Reasoned through three indexing schemes.

| Scheme | IDN cell count (upper bound) | Verdict |
|--------|------------------------------|---------|
| A. Pattern $\times$ observed sequence | 192 | Unusable without heavy merging |
| B. Observed sequence only | 56 | Manageable only with merging |
| C. Coarse observed type (5 buckets) | ~5 | Manageable |

Key econometric point: LCA is an individual-level restriction, so it holds within any well-defined cell, including MAR-weighted mixture cells from unbalanced observers. Adding observed-type cells does not break $\phi$ identification; it adds overidentifying restrictions. Extrapolation to balanced $d_N$ is unchanged because we still extrapolate using the balanced-cell $\mu_{d_N}$.

### The sleeper finding (paper/slides/unbalanced-pooled-cell-lca.pdf)

The current specification leaves both $\mu_{\mathrm{unb}}$ and $\Delta_{\mathrm{unb}}$ as free parameters. A5 (LCA) applies at the individual level, so it should also hold on the unbalanced pooled cell. Imposing

$$\Delta_{\mathrm{unb}} = \Delta_{\underline d_0} + \phi(\mu_{\mathrm{unb}} - \mu_{\underline d_0})$$

would drop $\Delta_{\mathrm{unb}}$ as free and let the unbalanced observers contribute to the identification of $\phi$ through the same LCA restriction as every switcher cell. One GMM constraint; one less free parameter. Built a 14-slide deck walking through this option. Added a third remark in the proposition pointing to this extension, framed as "we leave this to future work" pending a decision on whether to implement it.

### Phase 4 --- Verdier comparison

User asked how Verdier (2020) handles unbalanced panels. Verdier has a dedicated Appendix G in his online appendix. Read `papers/summaries/verdierAverageTreatmentEffects2020appendix.md` and the extracted Section G in `papers/extracted/verdierAverageTreatmentEffects2020appendix/paper.docling.md` lines 743--765.

Verdier's approach:
- Redefine stacked vectors $Y_i, W_i, Z_i, X_i$ to include only observed $(i,t)$ pairs.
- Redefine "movers" as individuals with treatment variation across observed periods.
- Step 1 regression: pool all observed observations, cross-section dummies + cross-section-dummies-$\times$-treatment + $z_{it}$. This is effectively an individual fixed-effects regression.
- Step 2 GMM: pool per-$t$ mover moments.
- MAR is stated unconditionally: $o_{it} \perp$ (all model variables).

Differences from CKT:
- Stronger MAR (unconditional vs. conditional on $x$).
- No partition of individuals into strata; no $U_i$ indicator.
- Group-level vs. individual-level intercept parameterization.

### Phase 5 --- Common-$\gamma$ question

User asked whether common-$\gamma$ applies across trajectories, not just across strata. Worked through the logic:
- A4 ($\gamma^U = \gamma^R$) says covariates affect consumption symmetrically across locations.
- A trajectory is a sequence of locations; so A4 implies common covariate effects across trajectory cells.
- Under MAR, the structural model carries over to unbalanced observers; so common-$\gamma$ across strata also follows from A4 + MAR.

Conclusion: Assumption~\ref{ass:common-gamma} in the proposition is formally a consequence of A4 + MAR, not new structure. It's stated separately so that the empirical test ($U_i \cdot x_{it}'\delta$ interaction + joint-$\delta=0$ Wald) is visible as a robustness check. Updated proposition to note this.

### Phase 6 --- Trajectory-dummy analysis

User asked whether we could just add trajectory dummies for unbalanced observers instead of the single pooled cell, how many new parameters that would require (worry: IDN explosion), and whether it would weaken extrapolation to stayers.

Three indexing schemes considered. Upper-bound cell counts for IDN (5 waves, 3+ observed):
- Scheme A (pattern $\times$ observed sequence): 32 + 80 + 80 = 192 cells.
- Scheme B (observed sequence only, pooled across patterns): 32 + 16 + 8 = 56 cells.
- Scheme C (coarse observed type, 5 buckets): ~5 cells.

Key econometric observation: LCA is an individual-level restriction, so it holds within any well-defined cell, including MAR-weighted mixture cells from unbalanced observers. Adding cells does not break $\phi$ identification; it adds overidentifying restrictions on $\phi$. Extrapolation to $d_N$ is unchanged because we still extrapolate using the balanced-cell $\mu_{d_N}$.

Wrote `docs/reviews/2026-04-23-unbalanced-trajectory-dummies.md` with this analysis.

### Phase 7 --- The sleeper finding

While reasoning through Part 5 of the dummy memo, noticed: the current specification has $(\alpha, \pi)$ as **both free parameters** --- the pooled unbalanced cell is LCA-unconstrained. But if A5 (LCA) holds at the individual level, it should hold on the pooled cell too: $\pi = \phi(\alpha - \mu_{\underline d_0})$. Imposing this drops $\pi$ and adds one moment for $\phi$. Half a day of Stata work, no new dummies.

This is strictly more principled than the current free-$\pi$ setup. If the restriction holds in the data, free. If it doesn't, the deviation is itself informative evidence (suggests MAR or A4 or A5 fails for unbalanced observers). Either way diagnostic value is high.

Added a new third remark in the proposition pointing to this extension, framed as "we leave this to future work" pending a decision.
