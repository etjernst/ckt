\begin{frame}{Our current estimator is Verdier's ``simple'' extrapolation}

  \textbf{What we currently do.} Following Suri (2011) and Tjernström (2023):
  \begin{itemize}
    \item impose the LCA restriction $\Deltai = \beta + \phicoef\, \thetai$;
    \item identify $\phicoef$ from switcher trajectories;
    \item extrapolate to non-migrants ($\dN$, $\dT$) via the LCA line.
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{0.6em}
  \textbf{What Verdier (2020) shows.} This procedure is one specific instance of a broader family.
  He calls our version the \alert{simple extrapolation} and proposes a \alert{robust} alternative that weakens one specific assumption.

  \vspace{0.6em}
  \textbf{What we propose.} Adopt Verdier's robust version.

\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The simple extrapolation assumes i.i.d.\ migration-cost shocks}

  Our current Assumption A2 states that the non-pecuniary utility shock
  \[
    \nuil \quad \text{is i.i.d.\ across individuals } i, \text{ time } t, \text{ and locations } l.
  \]

  \vspace{0.4em}
  \textbf{What this implies.} The residual migration-cost shifter is \emph{independent} of baseline skill and comparative advantage $(\tau_i, \thetai)$ \alert{everywhere in the sample}.

  \vspace{0.4em}
  \textbf{What this rules out.} Any systematic difference in the \emph{distribution} of migration-cost shocks across regions, institutions, or subpopulations.

  \vspace{0.6em}
  \begin{alertblock}{Practical reading}
    Under A2, two workers in completely different parts of the country face the same distribution of migration cost, conditional only on their own comparative advantage.
  \end{alertblock}

\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The migration literature documents exactly what A2 rules out}

  Regional variation in migration cost is the central fact of this literature:

  \vspace{0.6em}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Bryan and Morten (2019):} internal migration costs in Indonesia large enough to move Indonesia toward the world aggregate misallocation frontier.
    \item \textbf{Lagakos \textit{et al.}\ (2020):} migration costs vary substantially within and across developing countries.
    \item \textbf{Pulido and Swiecki (2021):} sector-side labor-market frictions correlate with regional productivity.
    \item \textbf{China specifically:} hukou institutions generate large, regionally-varying barriers to rural-to-urban migration.
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{0.6em}
  \textbf{Our current model rules this out by assumption.}

\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Verdier's robust extrapolation weakens A2 to a conditional version}

  \textbf{Proposal.} Decompose the migration-cost shock into a region-specific component and a residual:
  \[
    \nuil \;=\; m_{l}(\vii) \;+\; \tildenu,
  \]
  where $\vii$ is an observed, time-invariant individual-level index.

  \vspace{0.6em}
  \textbf{New assumption (A2$'$).} Only the residual $\tildenu$ is i.i.d.\ \emph{conditional on} $\vii$.
  The region-level component $m_{l}(\vii)$ can be arbitrarily correlated with baseline skill and comparative advantage.

  \vspace{0.6em}
  \textbf{What we pick for $\vii$.} First-wave province (same across all three countries, to start).

  \vspace{0.6em}
  \begin{alertblock}{Why this is weaker}
    A2 required independence of $\nuil$ from $(\tau_i,\thetai)$ unconditionally.
    A2$'$ requires only that the \emph{within-province} variation be independent.
  \end{alertblock}

\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The LCA gets a province-specific intercept; slope stays common}

  \textbf{Old LCA (simple extrapolation):}
  \[
    \Deltai \;=\; \beta \;+\; \phicoef\, \thetai.
  \]

  \vspace{0.3em}
  \textbf{New LCA (robust extrapolation):}
  \[
    \Deltai \;=\; \beta(\vii) \;+\; \phicoef\, \thetai.
  \]

  \vspace{0.6em}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Slope $\phicoef$ is \alert{common across provinces} --- this remains the LCA restriction.
    \item Intercept $\beta(\vii)$ absorbs province-level migration-cost heterogeneity.
    \item Identification of $\phicoef$ comes from switcher variation \emph{within} a province.
    \item Extrapolation to non-migrants happens province-by-province, then averaged.
  \end{itemize}

\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Pooling provinces mixes differentially-selected switcher samples}

  Consider two provinces:

  \vspace{0.4em}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Low-cost province} (coastal, good infrastructure). Migration cost low. Many types migrate. Switcher sample covers a wide range of $\thetai$.
    \item \textbf{High-cost province} (remote, poor infrastructure). Migration cost high. Only unusually high-return types migrate. Switcher sample is skewed.
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{0.6em}
  \textbf{Pooled regression} of $\Deltai$ on $\thetai$: biased by the across-province difference in selection.

  \vspace{0.4em}
  \textbf{Within-province regression}: restricts identification to comparisons where migration cost is held constant. Clean slope.

  \vspace{0.6em}
  \begin{alertblock}{This is what partialling out $\vii$ achieves}
    It purges the selection bias induced by region-level migration-cost heterogeneity.
  \end{alertblock}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Implementation adds $\vii$-interacted means to the GRC regression}

  \textbf{The restricted GRC, currently:}
  \begin{align*}
    y_{it} = \sum_{\underline{d}} \mud \mathbbm{1}\{\underline{d}_i = \underline{d}\}
            + \Delta_{\underline{d}_0} \D_{it}
            + \sum_{\underline{d}\in\DS\setminus\underline{d}_0} \phicoef(\mud - \mu_{\underline{d}_0}) \D_{it}\mathbbm{1}\{\underline{d}_i=\underline{d}\}
            + \cdots + \varepsilon_{it}.
  \end{align*}

  \vspace{0.3em}
  \textbf{Under the robust version:} trajectory means become trajectory $\times$ province means.
  \begin{align*}
    y_{it} = \sum_{\underline{d},v} \mu_{\underline{d},v}\, \mathbbm{1}\{\underline{d}_i=\underline{d}, \vii=v\}
            + \Delta_{\underline{d}_0}\D_{it}
            + \phicoef \sum_{\underline{d}\neq\underline{d}_0} (\mu_{\underline{d},\vii} - \mu_{\underline{d}_0,\vii})\D_{it}\mathbbm{1}\{\cdot\}
            + \cdots
  \end{align*}

  \vspace{0.4em}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Slope $\phicoef$ unchanged --- still a single scalar.
    \item Standard errors cluster at $\vii$.
    \item Stayer return $\Delta_{\dN}$ computed within each $\vii$-cluster, then averaged over clusters with switcher support.
  \end{itemize}

\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The cost-shifter diagnostic is a falsification test, not a substitute}

  Verdier also proposes a \alert{separate} diagnostic, using an observed continuous cost shifter $w_i$:

  \vspace{0.3em}
  \[
    \thetai^{*} = \alpha_0 + \alpha_1 \Deltai + \alpha_2 w_i + e_i,
    \qquad H_0: \alpha_2 = 0.
  \]

  In Kenya he uses $w_i = $ average distance to nearest seed seller.

  \vspace{0.6em}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Requires} an observed measured exogenous cost shifter.
    \item \textbf{Purpose:} falsify the simple extrapolation. If $\alpha_2 \neq 0$, the simple assumption is wrong.
    \item \textbf{Does not deliver} an alternative estimate on its own.
    \item \textbf{Not required} to implement the robust extrapolation; the robust version uses $\vii$ instead.
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{0.6em}
  \begin{alertblock}{For now}
    We focus on the robust extrapolation via $\vii = $ province.
    The cost-shifter test is a natural robustness addition if we can identify a defensible $w_i$ per country.
  \end{alertblock}

\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The move buys a weaker assumption; the costs are tractable}

  \textbf{What we gain.}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item A2$'$ is substantially weaker than A2 --- it matches what the migration literature documents.
    \item The CHN hukou split is now a principled special case (with $\vii = $ hukou) rather than an ad hoc fix.
    \item Stayer ATE estimates are defensible against the concern Verdier raised in Suri's own data.
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{0.4em}
  \textbf{What we give up.}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $\Delta_{\dN}$ is now an average over provinces with switcher support, not the full population average. (Feasibility survey will report support fractions.)
    \item Thinner identification of $\phicoef$: only within-province switcher variation contributes.
    \item Cluster-robust standard errors at the $\vii$ level.
  \end{itemize}

\end{frame}
