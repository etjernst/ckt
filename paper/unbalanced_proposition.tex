This appendix formalizes our treatment of individuals who are observed in fewer than $T_c$ waves.
The trajectory label $\underline d_i \in \mathcal D$ of Section~\ref{subsec:empirical-model} is defined only for individuals observed in all $T_c$ waves, so these unbalanced individuals have no trajectory label and cannot be assigned to the balanced-sample trajectory cells.
Rather than discard them, we pool them into a single additional cell parameterized by a rural-consumption mean $\mu_{\mathrm{unb}}$ and an average urban return $\Delta_{\mathrm{unb}}$ (parallel to the balanced-cell parameters $\mu_{\underline d}$ and $\Delta_{\underline d}$).
Proposition~\ref{prop:pooling} below shows that, under a missing-at-random condition on attrition and a common-covariate-effects condition across the two strata, the resulting two-step GMM estimator is $\sqrt n$-consistent and asymptotically normal for the trajectory parameters of interest, and weakly more efficient than the estimator restricted to the balanced sample.

Unbalanced individuals make up a substantial share of each country's estimation sample: between \textbf{[X\%]} and \textbf{[Y\%]} of individuals are observed in strictly fewer than $T_c$ waves.\footnote{Placeholder---shares of unbalanced individuals to be pulled from the country-specific summary-stats tables (Tables~\ref{tab:summary_stats_IDN_unb}, \ref{tab:summary_stats_CHN_unb}, \ref{tab:summary_stats_TZA_unb}). Do not confuse with the non-switcher shares (88.6\%--95.7\%) reported in the same tables.}
Let $U_i \equiv \mathbf 1\{|\mathcal T_i|<T_c\}$ indicate unbalanced status.
Augmenting the restricted GRC of Equation~\eqref{eq:restricted-GRC} with this pooled cell gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:restricted-grc-unbalanced}
\begin{aligned}
y_{it} =\; &
\sum_{\underline d \in \mathcal D \setminus \{d_T\}}
    \mu_{\underline d}\,\mathbbm 1\{\underline d_i=\underline d\}
+ \mu_{\mathrm{unb}}\,U_i
+ \Delta_{\underline d_0}\,D_{it} \\
& + \sum_{\underline d \in \mathcal D_S \setminus \{\underline d_0\}}
    \phi\,(\mu_{\underline d}-\mu_{\underline d_0})\,D_{it}\,
    \mathbbm 1\{\underline d_i=\underline d\} \\
& + \Big(\mu_{d_T}+\phi(\mu_{d_T}-\mu_{\underline d_0})\Big)\,D_{it}\,
    \mathbbm 1\{\underline d_i=d_T\} \\
& + \big(\Delta_{\mathrm{unb}}-\Delta_{\underline d_0}\big)\,U_i\,D_{it}
+ x_{it}'\gamma + \varepsilon_{it}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Because $\underline d_i$ is undefined when $U_i=1$, the trajectory indicators $\mathbbm 1\{\underline d_i=\underline d\}$ are identically zero on the unbalanced subsample.
For an unbalanced individual, the equation collapses to $y_{it} = \mu_{\mathrm{unb}} + \Delta_{\mathrm{unb}} D_{it} + x_{it}'\gamma + \varepsilon_{it}$, so $\mu_{\mathrm{unb}}$ and $\Delta_{\mathrm{unb}}$ are directly the rural-consumption level and the average urban return on the unbalanced stratum.
The single coefficient vector $\gamma$ imposes that covariate effects are common across the two strata.
In the Stata implementation, $\mu_{\mathrm{unb}}$ is the coefficient on the unbalanced dummy and $\Delta_{\mathrm{unb}} - \Delta_{\underline d_0}$ is the coefficient on its interaction with the urban indicator.
\citet{verdierAverageTreatmentEffects2020a} discusses a related unbalanced-panel extension in Appendix~G of his online appendix that redefines stacked vectors over observed periods; we adopt the pooled-cell specification instead because it preserves the trajectory-cell structure of Section~\ref{subsec:restricted-grc-model}.

\begin{assumption}[Missing at random, conditional on covariates]
\label{ass:mar}
$U_i \perp (\theta_i,\tau_i,\{\nu_{it}^l\}_{t,l}) \mid \{x_{it}\}_{t\in\mathcal T_i}$.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{ass:mar} is the standard ignorability condition for unbalanced panels in the tradition of \citet{rubin1976inference} and \citet[ch.~17-19]{wooldridge2010econometric}: non-response depends at most on covariates already included in the regression, and the joint distribution of latent productivities and shocks on the unbalanced stratum is, conditional on $x$, the same as on the balanced stratum.
The rich demographic controls in CFPS, IFLS, and TZNPS make this plausible.\footnote{\textcolor{red}{TODO: verify the following claim before submission. See \texttt{docs/reviews/2026-04-23-todo-before-submission.md}.} \textcolor{red}{If panel entry cohorts matter for selection beyond what age, education, and household composition capture, wave-of-first-observation indicators can be added to $x_{it}$ without changing any of the arguments that follow. The version of the estimator reported in the main tables does not include such indicators; results are essentially unchanged when they are added (available on request).}}
Section~\ref{sec:robustness} probes the assumption empirically by comparing balanced-only and pooled estimates.

\begin{assumption}[Common covariate effects]
\label{ass:common-gamma}
The partial effect of $x_{it}$ on log consumption is the same in the balanced and unbalanced strata.
\end{assumption}

Equation~\eqref{eq:restricted-grc-unbalanced} encodes Assumption~\ref{ass:common-gamma} through the single coefficient vector $\gamma$.
The assumption is directly testable: replace $x_{it}'\gamma$ with $x_{it}'\gamma + U_i\,x_{it}'\delta$ and test $\delta=0$.
Assumption~\ref{ass:common-gamma} is a consequence of Assumption~(A4) ($\gamma^U=\gamma^R$) from Section~\ref{subsec:empirical-model} combined with Assumption~\ref{ass:mar}; we state it separately so that the corresponding empirical test is available as a robustness check.

\begin{proposition}[Pooling balanced and unbalanced individuals]
\label{prop:pooling}
Let
\[
\vartheta_T \equiv \big(\Delta_{\underline d_0},\,\phi,\,
\{\mu_{\underline d}\}_{\underline d \in \mathcal D\setminus\{d_T\}},\,
\mu_{d_T}+\phi(\mu_{d_T}-\mu_{\underline d_0})\big)
\]
denote the trajectory-block parameters, and let $\theta_0 \equiv (\vartheta_T,\mu_{\mathrm{unb}},\Delta_{\mathrm{unb}},\gamma)$ denote the full parameter vector of \eqref{eq:restricted-grc-unbalanced}.
Under Assumptions~(A1)--(A5) of Section~\ref{subsec:empirical-model} and Assumptions~\ref{ass:mar}--\ref{ass:common-gamma}, the two-step GMM estimator of \eqref{eq:restricted-grc-unbalanced} is $\sqrt n$-consistent and asymptotically normal for $\theta_0$.
The trajectory-specific returns $\Delta_{\underline d} = \Delta_{\underline d_0} + \phi(\mu_{\underline d}-\mu_{\underline d_0})$ inherit consistency and asymptotic normality by the delta method.
Because the balanced-only estimator does not identify $(\mu_{\mathrm{unb}},\Delta_{\mathrm{unb}})$, the efficiency comparison applies to the sub-vector shared by the two estimators: the pooled asymptotic variance of $(\hat\vartheta_T,\hat\gamma)$ is weakly smaller than the balanced-only analogue, with strict inequality whenever the unbalanced stratum is informative about $\gamma$ and the trajectory score is correlated with the covariate score.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The argument has three steps: validity of the pooled moment conditions (Step 1); identification and standard non-linear GMM asymptotics (Step 2); and a partitioned-inverse argument for the efficiency comparison (Step 3).

\textbf{Step 1 (moment validity).}
Let the individual-period moment function be $g_{it}(\theta) \equiv z_{it}\cdot\varepsilon_{it}(\theta)$, with $z_{it}$ collecting the trajectory indicators $\mathbbm 1\{\underline d_i=\underline d\}$, the urban indicator $D_{it}$, the switcher interactions $D_{it}\cdot\mathbbm 1\{\underline d_i=\underline d\}$, the pair $(U_i,\,U_i D_{it})$, and the covariates $x_{it}$.
For inference under individual-level clustering we stack within individual, $g_i(\theta) \equiv \sum_{t\in\mathcal T_i} g_{it}(\theta)$, and treat $\{g_i\}_{i=1}^n$ as i.i.d.\ across $i$; the efficient weighting matrix $\hat S^{-1}$ estimates the variance of $g_i$ across $i$, so all asymptotic variances that follow are cluster-robust at the individual level, consistent with the main paper's inference procedure.
The population moment condition is $E[g_i(\theta_0)] = 0$.
We verify this stratum by stratum.

On the balanced subsample ($U_i=0$), Assumptions~(A1)--(A5) give $E[\varepsilon_{it}\mid\underline d_i, D_{it}, x_{it}, U_i=0] = 0$ at each observed $t$, which delivers every moment involving the trajectory indicators, $D_{it}$, the switcher interactions, and $x_{it}$; the moments involving $U_i$ and $U_i D_{it}$ are identically zero.

On the unbalanced subsample ($U_i=1$), the switcher-interaction moments $D_{it}\cdot\mathbbm 1\{\underline d_i=\underline d\}$ and the trajectory-indicator moments $\mathbbm 1\{\underline d_i=\underline d\}$ are identically zero because $\mathbbm 1\{\underline d_i=\underline d\}\equiv 0$ when $U_i=1$.
Three moments remain and must be zero at $\theta_0$: those involving $(U_i,\,U_i D_{it},\,x_{it})$ and the bare $D_{it}$ moment, which is non-degenerate on this stratum.
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:mar}, the conditional distribution of $(D_{it},\theta_i,\tau_i,\{\nu_{it}^l\})$ given $x_{it}$ is the same on both strata.
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:common-gamma}, the partial effect of $x_{it}$ on $y_{it}$ is likewise common.
The pair $(\mu_{\mathrm{unb},0},\Delta_{\mathrm{unb},0})$ is therefore the unique pair of scalars for which
\[
E\big[y_{it} - \mu_{\mathrm{unb},0} - \Delta_{\mathrm{unb},0} D_{it} - x_{it}'\gamma \,\big|\, U_i=1,\,D_{it},\,x_{it}\big] = 0,
\]
with $\mu_{\mathrm{unb},0} = E[\alpha_i^R \mid U_i=1]$ absorbing the unbalanced-stratum rural level and $\Delta_{\mathrm{unb},0} = E[\Delta_i \mid U_i=1]$ the unbalanced-stratum average return.
Both are well-defined population quantities under Assumptions~\ref{ass:mar}--\ref{ass:common-gamma}.
It follows that $E[\varepsilon_{it}\mid U_i=1,D_{it},x_{it}]=0$ at $\theta_0$, which delivers the $(U_i,\,U_i D_{it},\,x_{it})$ moments and the bare $D_{it}$ moment on the unbalanced stratum.
Combining the two strata, $E[g_i(\theta_0)]=0$.

\textbf{Step 2 (identification and asymptotics).}
The balanced-block moments identify $\vartheta_T$ as argued in Section~\ref{subsec:restricted-grc-model}: the trajectory-indicator moments identify $\{\mu_{\underline d}\}$ as fixed effects in the regression, and the LCA restriction imposed across switcher-interaction moments identifies $\phi$ together with $\Delta_{\underline d_0}$.
Identification of $\phi$ requires at least two switcher trajectories with distinct trajectory means, $\exists\,\underline d,\underline d'\in\mathcal D_S$ with $\mu_{\underline d}\neq\mu_{\underline d'}$, which is assumed throughout and satisfied in all three panels.
Equation~\eqref{eq:phi-proposition1} gives one convenient interpretation of $\phi$ as the ratio of cross-trajectory return differences to cross-trajectory mean differences; in the GMM estimator, $\phi$ is identified jointly from all switcher-interaction moments under the LCA restriction.
The $(U_i,U_iD_{it})$ block identifies $(\mu_{\mathrm{unb}},\Delta_{\mathrm{unb}})$, and the $x_{it}$ block identifies $\gamma$.
Under the usual regularity conditions---compact parameter space, continuous differentiability of $g_{it}$ in $\theta$, global identification of $\theta_0$, and finite fourth moments of $(z_{it},\varepsilon_{it})$---the standard non-linear GMM arguments \citep{hansen1982gmm,neweyMcFadden1994} deliver $\sqrt n$-consistency and asymptotic normality of $\hat\theta$.
Consistency and asymptotic normality of $\hat\Delta_{\underline d} = \hat\Delta_{\underline d_0} + \hat\phi(\hat\mu_{\underline d}-\hat\mu_{\underline d_0})$ then follow by the delta method.

\textbf{Step 3 (efficiency).}
Write the efficient-weight asymptotic information matrix for the sub-vector $(\vartheta_T,\gamma)$ as
\[
I = \begin{pmatrix} I_{\vartheta_T\vartheta_T} & I_{\vartheta_T\gamma} \\
I_{\gamma\vartheta_T} & I_{\gamma\gamma} \end{pmatrix},
\]
where blocks involving $(\mu_{\mathrm{unb}},\Delta_{\mathrm{unb}})$ are handled separately because those parameters are not targets of the efficiency comparison.
Partitioned inversion gives $\mathrm{AVar}(\hat\vartheta_T) = \big(I_{\vartheta_T\vartheta_T} - I_{\vartheta_T\gamma}I_{\gamma\gamma}^{-1}I_{\gamma\vartheta_T}\big)^{-1}$.
Because $\{g_i\}$ are i.i.d.\ across individuals, $I_{\gamma\gamma}$ is the sum of per-individual $x$-block information contributions; the unbalanced stratum adds a positive-semidefinite block under Assumptions~\ref{ass:mar}--\ref{ass:common-gamma}, so the pooled $I_{\gamma\gamma}$ is weakly larger than the balanced-only analogue, and $I_{\gamma\gamma}^{-1}$ is weakly smaller.
The trajectory-block information $I_{\vartheta_T\vartheta_T}$ is unchanged between pooled and balanced-only estimators, because the trajectory-indicator and switcher-interaction scores vanish identically on the unbalanced subsample.
It follows that the correction $I_{\vartheta_T\gamma}I_{\gamma\gamma}^{-1}I_{\gamma\vartheta_T}$ is weakly smaller under pooling and $\mathrm{AVar}(\hat\vartheta_T)$ is weakly smaller.
The inequality is strict whenever $I_{\vartheta_T\gamma}\neq 0$ and the unbalanced stratum contributes strictly to $I_{\gamma\gamma}$, i.e., whenever the covariates are informative about the trajectory parameters and the unbalanced sample is non-degenerate.
The same partitioned-inverse argument delivers a weak efficiency improvement for $\hat\gamma$ itself.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Because the trajectory indicators are identically zero for unbalanced individuals, the unbalanced subsample does not contribute directly to the identification of any switcher return; it enters \eqref{eq:restricted-grc-unbalanced} only through the estimation of $\gamma$ (which subsumes period effects) and the pooled pair $(\mu_{\mathrm{unb}},\Delta_{\mathrm{unb}})$.
If Assumption~\ref{ass:mar} fails on the unbalanced stratum, the bias operates only through $\hat\gamma$; the trajectory estimates $\hat\Delta_{\underline d}$ inherit bias only to the extent that $\hat\gamma^{\mathrm{pool}}$ does and to the extent that the trajectory-indicator scores are correlated with the covariate scores.
We do not claim full robustness to non-ignorable attrition---the proof does not deliver that---but the block structure of the score makes the pooled trajectory estimates less sensitive to unbalanced-stratum misspecification than an estimator that let the unbalanced individuals contribute directly to trajectory identification would be.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
The current parameterization leaves $\Delta_{\mathrm{unb}}$ unconstrained by LCA.
Because A5 is an individual-level restriction that applies to every worker, the pooled unbalanced cell should itself satisfy the cell-level LCA $\Delta_{\mathrm{unb}} = \Delta_{\underline d_0} + \phi(\mu_{\mathrm{unb}}-\mu_{\underline d_0})$.
Imposing this relation as an additional moment condition would eliminate $\Delta_{\mathrm{unb}}$ as a free parameter and allow the unbalanced individuals to contribute to the identification of $\phi$ through the same LCA restriction that governs balanced switcher cells.
We leave this extension to future work; the unrestricted specification reported here is the conservative choice.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
The balanced/pooled gap in estimates of $\Delta_{\underline d}$, reported in Tables~\ref{tab:GRC_IDN_consumption_urban_bal}, \ref{tab:GRC_CHN_consumption_urban_bal}, and \ref{tab:GRC_TZA_consumption_urban_bal}, is a direct empirical check on the joint null that Assumptions~\ref{ass:mar} and \ref{ass:common-gamma} both hold.
Systematic divergence would signal either misspecified covariate effects across strata or a violation of MAR that contaminates $\hat\gamma^{\mathrm{pool}}$.
The close correspondence across CFPS, IFLS, and TZNPS is consistent with the joint null.
Separating the two conditions would require additional structure.
\end{remark}
