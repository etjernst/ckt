The estimation sample is unbalanced: in country $c$ with wave count
$T_c \in\{3,4,5\}$, between $88.6\%$ and $95.7\%$ of individuals are
observed in strictly fewer than $T_c$ waves. Following
Section~\ref{subsec:empirical-model}, a trajectory label
$\underline d_i\in\mathcal{D}$ is defined only for individuals observed
in all $T_c$ waves. Unbalanced observers are not assigned a trajectory.
Rather than discarding them, we pool them into the estimating equation
through a single indicator $U_i\equiv\mathbf{1}\{|\mathcal{T}_i|<T_c\}$,
interacted with the urban dummy:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:restricted-grc-unbalanced}
\begin{aligned}
y_{it} = \; &
\sum_{\underline d \in \mathcal{D}} \mu_{\underline d}\,
\mathbbm{1}\{\underline d_i=\underline d\}
+
\sum_{\underline d \in \mathcal{D}_S} \Delta_{\underline d}\,
\mathbbm{1}\{\underline d_i=\underline d\}\, D_{it} \\
& +\, \alpha\, U_i
+ \pi\, U_i\, D_{it}
+ x_{it}'\gamma
+ \varepsilon_{it},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with the linear comparative advantage restriction
$\Delta_{\underline d}=\beta+\phi\,E[\theta_i\mid\underline d_i=\underline d]$
imposed on the switcher coefficients as in the restricted GRC of
Section~\ref{subsec:empirical-model}. Because $\underline d_i$ is
undefined when $U_i=1$, the indicators
$\{\mathbbm{1}\{\underline d_i=\underline d\}\}_{\underline d\in\mathcal{D}}$
are identically zero on the unbalanced subsample. The pair
$(\alpha, \pi)$ absorbs the level and the average urban return of the
unbalanced stratum through a single pooled cell.

\begin{assumption}[Missing at random, conditional on covariates]
\label{ass:mar}
$U_i \perp (\theta_i, \tau_i, \{\nu_{it}^l\}_{t,l})\mid x_{i1},\dots,x_{iT_c}$.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{ass:mar} requires that non-response depends at most on
observed covariates. We view this as defensible in the CFPS, IFLS, and
TZNPS samples given the rich demographic controls, and probe it
directly through the balanced-panel comparison in
Section~\ref{sec:robustness}.

\begin{proposition}[Pooling balanced and unbalanced observers]
\label{prop:pooling}
Under the identifying assumptions of Section~\ref{subsec:empirical-model}
and Assumption~\ref{ass:mar}, the two-step GMM estimator of
\eqref{eq:restricted-grc-unbalanced} is consistent and asymptotically
normal for $(\beta,\phi,\gamma)$ and for the collection of
balanced-panel trajectory parameters
$\{(\mu_{\underline d},\Delta_{\underline d})\}_{\underline d\in\mathcal{D}}$.
Its asymptotic variance is weakly smaller than that of the
corresponding estimator restricted to balanced observers only.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The argument has two steps: an orthogonality step that isolates the
contribution of balanced observers to
$\{(\mu_{\underline d},\Delta_{\underline d})\}$, and a consistency
step for the covariate and nuisance parameters.

\emph{Step 1 (orthogonality).}
By construction, $\mathbbm{1}\{\underline d_i=\underline d\}=0$ for
every $\underline d\in\mathcal{D}$ whenever $U_i=1$, because the
trajectory label is only defined on the balanced subsample. Hence for
every $\underline d\in\mathcal{D}$ and every wave $t$,
\begin{equation*}
\mathbbm{1}\{\underline d_i=\underline d\}\cdot U_i = 0,
\qquad
\mathbbm{1}\{\underline d_i=\underline d\}\cdot U_i D_{it} = 0.
\end{equation*}
The design matrix therefore block-separates: the balanced regressors
$W_1 \equiv \bigl(\mathbbm{1}\{\underline d_i=\underline d\},\,
\mathbbm{1}\{\underline d_i=\underline d\}\, D_{it}\bigr)_{\underline
d\in\mathcal{D}}$ are orthogonal in the sample to the unbalanced
controls $W_2\equiv(U_i,\, U_i D_{it})$. By the
Frisch--Waugh--Lovell theorem, the GMM estimator of $(\mu,\Delta)$ is
numerically equal to the estimator obtained by first partialling out
$W_2$ and $x_{it}$, then fitting the residuals to the residualised
$W_1$ using only the balanced subsample.

\emph{Step 2 (consistency of the partialling-out).}
Step~1 requires consistent estimation of $(\gamma,\alpha,\pi)$ from
the pooled sample. Under the time-invariant comparative advantage and
i.i.d. shock assumptions, the conditional mean of $y_{it}$ given
$(\underline d_i, D_{it}, U_i, x_{it})$ separates additively as in
\eqref{eq:restricted-grc-unbalanced}. Under
Assumption~\ref{ass:mar}, $U_i$ carries no information about
$(\theta_i,\tau_i,\nu)$ beyond what $x_{it}$ already contains, so
$E[\varepsilon_{it}\mid\underline d_i, D_{it}, U_i, x_{it}]=0$ on both
strata. Standard GMM arguments \citep{hansen1982gmm} deliver
$\sqrt{n}$-consistency and asymptotic normality of the full parameter
vector in \eqref{eq:restricted-grc-unbalanced}, including the balanced
$(\mu_{\underline d},\Delta_{\underline d})$.

\emph{Step 3 (efficiency).}
Let $\hat\gamma^{\mathrm{pool}}$ denote the covariate coefficient
estimated on the full sample and $\hat\gamma^{\mathrm{bal}}$ the
analogous estimator on balanced observers only. Under
Assumption~\ref{ass:mar}, both are consistent for $\gamma$, and
$\hat\gamma^{\mathrm{pool}}$ has weakly smaller asymptotic variance
because it uses strictly more observations. The partialling-out
residual $M_{W_2,x}y$ that enters Step~1 therefore has weakly smaller
variance under pooling, which carries over to
$(\mu_{\underline d},\Delta_{\underline d})$ via the sandwich variance
formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The orthogonality of Step~1 is the substantive content of the
proposition. Because the trajectory indicators are identically zero
for unbalanced observers, the unbalanced subsample does not contribute
directly to the identification of any $\Delta_{\underline d}$ of
economic interest; it enters
\eqref{eq:restricted-grc-unbalanced} only through the estimation of
$\gamma$, period shifters, and the pooled pair $(\alpha,\pi)$. As a
result, the balanced-panel estimates are robust to substantially
stronger forms of mis-specification for the unbalanced stratum than
Assumption~\ref{ass:mar} requires: even if attrition were
non-ignorable on the unbalanced subsample, the bias would operate only
through $\hat\gamma$, and the block structure of the score implies
that the induced bias in $\hat\Delta_{\underline d}$ is of smaller
order whenever $\hat\gamma^{\mathrm{pool}}\overset{p}{\to}\gamma$.
The pooled specification is therefore conservative: it never loses
information relative to the balanced-only estimator and is weakly more
efficient whenever $\gamma$ is correctly specified in both strata.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
An immediate corollary is that the empirical gap between the
balanced-only and pooled estimates of $\Delta_{\underline d}$,
reported in Tables~\ref{tab:GRC_IDN_consumption_urban_bal},
\ref{tab:GRC_CHN_consumption_urban_bal}, and
\ref{tab:GRC_TZA_consumption_urban_bal}, provides a direct empirical
check on Assumption~\ref{ass:mar}: systematic divergence would signal
either mis-specified covariate effects across strata or a violation of
MAR that contaminates $\hat\gamma^{\mathrm{pool}}$. The close
correspondence we observe is consistent with both conditions holding
in the CFPS, IFLS, and TZNPS panels.
\end{remark}
