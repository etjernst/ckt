# Step 1: atomic claims

Target citation: `herrendorfWagesHumanCapital2018`.

Proposed description under audit: Herrendorf and Schoellman combine cross-country census evidence on sectoral wages and human capital with panel evidence on workers leaving agriculture in the United States, Brazil, and Indonesia; the switcher wage gains are positive but small relative to sectoral wage gaps and support a selection-based interpretation with limited barriers to labor mobility.

1. C1, sample-scope: The paper studies sectoral wages and human capital across 13 countries.
   Verification question: What countries and data sources form the paper's cross-country sample?
2. C2, empirical: The paper documents lower wages, education, and Mincer returns in agriculture than in nonagricultural sectors.
   Verification question: What sectoral differences in wages, schooling, and Mincer returns does the paper document?
3. C3, sample-scope: The paper uses or synthesizes panel evidence on workers leaving agriculture in the United States, Brazil, and Indonesia.
   Verification question: Which countries enter the paper's panel evidence on workers leaving agriculture, and which estimates are the authors' own?
4. C4, quantitative-framing: The wage gains for switchers are positive but small relative to raw and human-capital-adjusted sectoral wage gaps.
   Verification question: How do the reported switcher wage gains compare with raw and adjusted sectoral wage gaps?
5. C5, framing: The panel evidence is closer to the model's selection view than to its technology view.
   Verification question: Which model interpretation do the authors say is closer to the panel evidence?
6. C6, framing: The paper concludes that barriers to labor reallocation are smaller than the macro-development literature had argued.
   Verification question: What does the paper conclude about the magnitude of barriers to leaving agriculture?
