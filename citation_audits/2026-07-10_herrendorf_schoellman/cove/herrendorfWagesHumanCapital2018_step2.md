# Step 2: primary-source verification

Source 1: Herrendorf and Schoellman, author-posted April 2017 resubmission, `AgWagesPaperResubmission.pdf`.

Source 2: American Economic Association article page for the published 2018 article.

1. C1: SUPPORTED.
   Evidence: The abstract and introduction state that the sample contains 13 countries and 42 IPUMS population censuses, ranging from Canada and the United States to India and Indonesia (author PDF, pp. 1--2; AEA abstract).
2. C2: SUPPORTED.
   Evidence: The abstract states that average wages are considerably lower in agriculture and that agricultural workers have less education and lower Mincer returns (author PDF, p. 1; AEA abstract).
3. C3: SUPPORTED WITH QUALIFIER.
   Evidence: The introduction says that the authors estimate switcher gains using the US PSID and complement that estimate with Alvarez's Brazil estimate and Hicks et al.'s Indonesia estimate; Section 5.2 tabulates all three (author PDF, pp. 2--3 and 22).
   Qualifier: Only the US panel estimate is the authors' own; the Brazil and Indonesia estimates come from other studies.
4. C4: SUPPORTED.
   Evidence: The introduction states that gains are positive but small relative to raw and human-capital-adjusted wage gaps, and Table 5 reports switcher gain ratios of 1.06 for the United States, 1.01--1.12 for Brazil, and 1.05 for Indonesia against larger raw and adjusted gaps (author PDF, pp. 2--3 and 22).
5. C5: SUPPORTED.
   Evidence: The authors state that the barriers implied by switcher gains are close to their selection-view values and far from their technology-view values (author PDF, pp. 3 and 22).
6. C6: SUPPORTED.
   Evidence: The published abstract says that implied barriers are considerably smaller than the macro-development literature had argued (AEA abstract).
