## Decisions, with the why

- Decision: report the urban rate as the headline rank-condition diagnostic, the residual share as the partialling check, and the within-switch share as a credibility supplement.
Why: the urban rate is the necessary-and-sufficient sub-block condition (clean for a methods reader); the residual share closes the partialling gap that the second reviewer surfaced; the within-switch share gives a stricter, selection-resistant signal that survives partial misspecification of the orthogonality condition.
The three answer different questions and the cleanest package reports all three.
- Decision: rename macros from `\unbTreatRate*` / `\unbPartialRsq*` to `\unbUrbanRate*` / `\unbResidShare*`.
Why: the user pushed back that "treatment" framing is wrong because $D_{it}$ is the urban indicator, not a randomized intervention.
"Partial $R^2$" was renamed to `ResidShare` because the underlying number is $1-R^2$ (the residualized share) and the "partial $R^2$" label was misleading on the directionality (a "high partial $R^2$" reads as bad in standard usage but the prose wanted "high = good").
- Decision: extend `1b_unbalanced_rank_diagnostic.do` to compute the always-rural / always-urban / switcher decomposition even though only the within-switch share originally landed in the appendix prose.
Why: the v1 reviewer flagged that with only 182 switchers and 3,170 unbalanced individuals in TZA, the reader cannot tell from headline numbers alone whether between-individual variation comes from a meaningful number of always-urban unbalanced individuals or from one or two outliers.
The decomposition (TZA: 1,968 always-rural, 1,020 always-urban, 182 switchers) settles the question.
- Decision: tighten the partition-violation check from `di as error` to `error 9999`.
Why: `capture noisily` only catches hard Stata errors, not display commands.
A silent partition violation would have written incorrect macros and the only trace would have been an easy-to-miss line in the log.
- Decision: drop `quietly` on the partial-$R^2$ regress; log estimation N immediately after.
Why: listwise deletion of missing covariates and silent factor-level drops would otherwise be invisible.
The estimation N (CHN 52,680 / IDN 76,607 / TZA 6,338) confirms the regression sample is sensible.
- Decision: skip the methods-critic suggestion to pre-check `i.period` for degenerate expansion.
Why: user judgment---the data definitionally have multiple periods in the unbalanced stratum (an unbalanced individual must by construction have $\geq 2$ observed periods unless they are observed in exactly one wave, which the `data_setup` program already drops via the `obs_per_individual >= 2` keep).
- Decision: hold off on the long-version's `[X\%] / [Y\%]` placeholder for the share of unbalanced individuals.
Why: this is a different statistic from the rank diagnostic (sample composition, not identification), and the long version is no longer the leading candidate for the submission anyway.
Tracked as an open item rather than implemented.
- Decision: keep the long version of the appendix as supplementary material rather than deleting once the short version is adopted.
Why: user said "doesn't cost much"---low-cost optionality for the submission process.
