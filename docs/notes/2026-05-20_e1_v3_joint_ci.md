## What changed since the 2026-05-18 smoke

The smoke driver passed `log(consumption)` and no controls to `fit_auxiliary_ols`.
The Wald-at-GMM diagnostic on 2026-05-20 localized that as the bug.
This run matches the GMM cuu_ca spec: outcome is $\log(\text{consumption} / \text{hhsize\_cube})$, controls are period FE + female + age² + education + education², and the sample drops `mi(education_max)`, `mi(age)`, and `obs_per_individual == 1`.
The lattice was refined from $121 \times 51$ to $301 \times 101$ (0.01 spacing on both axes).

## What works

The construction is now sound.
The Wald at the GMM point falls well below threshold for both countries (24.2 vs 40.1 for IDN; 3.1 vs 11.1 for TZA), and the joint CI contains the GMM point in a single connected component.
Refining the lattice to 0.01 spacing materially shrinks the rounding error in the marginal projections.

## What does not work yet

**Möbius pole crossing.**
Both countries have accepted regions extending past $\phi = -1$ (IDN goes to $\phi = -1.21$; TZA to $-1.37$).
The aggregate's $\Delta_{d_T}$ piece passes through the Möbius singularity at $\phi = -1$ from below.
The TZA convex hull on the misallocation gap inflates to $+171\%$, which is not a credible upper bound; it is the pole.
The plan's P3 fallback (drop the $d_T$ piece, report the aggregate as $d_N$ plus switchers only) is the right remedy and is not yet implemented.
With P3 the TZA upper bound should fall back into a defensible range.

**`project_image_intervals` binning artifact.**
The image projection reports 29--42 islands per country, but the underlying accepted region has 1 connected component.
The fragmentation is a one-bin-per-value artifact at low $N$, flagged in the prior session log.
The convex hull is the right summary until the binning is fixed; the per-interval listing is misleading.

**Residual upper-bound disagreement with 5b.**
Joint-CI marginal $\phi$ projection for IDN is $[-1.210, +0.900]$ against 5b's attached $[-1.23, -0.01]$.
The lower bounds match within rounding; the upper bound is materially wider in Python ($+0.90$ vs $-0.01$).
Same disagreement appears in the K-1-dof marginal CI from `grid_lca_inversion`, so it is upstream of `build_joint_ci_grid`.
Likely a deeper Stata-vs-Python difference in `set_covariates` drops, the `unbalanced` indicator's interaction with the OLS, or the `attach_inversion_ci` inversion procedure itself.
Does not block the V3 plumbing; flagged for follow-up.

## CHN treatment in E1

Pooled CHN (`grc_CHN_cuu_ca`) cannot enter E1 directly because the J-test rejects on the pooled sample.
CHN enters E1 through the hukou-split sters once their inversion scalars are attached.
Reporting convention agreed on 2026-05-20: report CHN E1 in two forms.
First, the RF and UF aggregates separately as two counterfactuals (one per regime, each with its own joint CI on the misallocation gap).
Second, an RF + UF weighted aggregate using the rural-first and urban-first population shares in the CHN sample (the "national" CHN misallocation gap, treating the two regimes as separate components of a single national counterfactual).
We will look at both and decide on the headline once we see the magnitudes.
