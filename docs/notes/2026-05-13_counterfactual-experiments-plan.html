Counterfactual experiments to develop

Four exercises to convert the paper's identified parameters into magnitudes the audience can press on.

2026-05-13 · branch lca-inversion

TL;DR

The paper currently reports identification and estimates but not magnitudes; these four counterfactuals turn $(\beta, \phi)$ and the LCA extrapolation into per-worker and aggregate consumption numbers, and two of them are implementable with the inversion CI machinery already in hand.

Design notes after the 2026-05-18 methods review; see the implementation plan for the build sequence.

Why these counterfactuals

Pro-poor returns ($\phi<0$) is a coefficient. The non-migrant extrapolation is reported through the slope and intercept of $\Delta_i = \beta + \phi\theta_i$ rather than as “the average never-migrant in country $c$ would gain $X$ log points.” The exercises below convert the identified objects into readable magnitudes that align with the paper's stated contribution.

1. Aggregate misallocation accounting Ready to implement

What is aggregate log consumption under optimal sorting versus observed sorting, expressed as a percentage of observed consumption?

Identified objects (in hand)
  • $\pi_{\underline{d}}$, $\mu_{\underline{d}}$, $\bar{D}_{\underline{d}}$ from the data
  • $\Delta_{\underline{d}}$ for switchers from the unrestricted GRC
  • $\Delta_{d_T}$ from the LCA inversion
  • $\Delta_{d_N} = \beta + \phi(\mu_{d_N} - \mu_{\underline{d}_0})$ from LCA extrapolation
Computation

Per-worker gain from optimal sort: $\max(0, \Delta_{\underline{d}}) - \Delta_{\underline{d}} \bar{D}_{\underline{d}}$.

Aggregate gap: sum across trajectories weighted by $\pi_{\underline{d}}$.

Headline output

Per country, the misallocation gap as a percentage of observed aggregate consumption, decomposed into pieces from $d_N$, switchers, and $d_T$.

Inference

For each $(\phi, \beta)$ in the inversion CI, recompute the gap; report the convex hull. Reuses the inversion CI machinery directly.

$$\text{Misallocation gap} = \sum_{\underline{d}} \pi_{\underline{d}} \left[\max(0, \Delta_{\underline{d}}) - \Delta_{\underline{d}} \bar{D}_{\underline{d}}\right]$$

Where the action is. The $d_N$ piece dominates because $\pi_{d_N}$ is large in every country (back-of-envelope: $\approx 0.75$ TZA, $\approx 0.65$ CHN, $\approx 0.50$ IDN; shares to verify against descriptives) and $\bar{D}_{d_N} = 0$. The misallocation number is essentially “what would never-migrants gain from migrating, on average, weighted by their share.”

Both extremes: no further migration as the lower bound

The misallocation gap measures observed sorting versus optimal sorting. Pairing it with the converse---observed sorting versus a no-further-migration scenario in which workers stay where their first observed period puts them---brackets where observed migration sits in the range of possibilities. The intensive-margin reading is deliberate: the counterfactual fixes subsequent location switching at zero but does not undo extensive-margin migration that occurred before the panel begins, so it isolates the value of within-panel location switching rather than the value of migration over a lifetime. Let $W_{\text{obs}}$, $W_{\text{nofm}}$, $W_{\text{opt}}$ denote aggregate log consumption under the three scenarios.

$$\underbrace{W_{\text{obs}} - W_{\text{nofm}}}_{\text{value of observed migration}} = \sum_{\underline{d}} \pi_{\underline{d}} \cdot \Delta_{\underline{d}} \cdot \bar{D}_{\underline{d}}; \qquad \underbrace{W_{\text{opt}} - W_{\text{obs}}}_{\text{misallocation gap}} = \sum_{\underline{d}} \pi_{\underline{d}} \left[\max(0, \Delta_{\underline{d}}) - \Delta_{\underline{d}} \bar{D}_{\underline{d}}\right]$$

The no-further-migration calculation is cleaner than the optimal-sort one: the $d_N$ piece drops out ($\bar{D}_{d_N} = 0$), and within-trajectory heterogeneity in $\Delta_i$ does not enter because everyone in a trajectory shares the same $\bar{D}$. Only conditional means are needed, all identified by the GRC. The switcher piece uses the non-parametric $\Delta_{\underline{d}}$ from the unrestricted GRC; the $d_T$ piece uses $\Delta_{d_T}$ from the LCA inversion.

Compute regime by regime once the hukou-split tables are extended to include $\Delta_{d_T}$ rows.

Implication. Combining the two extremes: observed migration delivers no detectable net welfare gain in TZA or IDN under the LCA point estimates, while optimal migration would deliver $\approx 22\%$ (TZA) or $\approx 3.7\%$ (IDN). The misallocation gap is essentially the entire potential value of within-panel migration. The lower bound is fragile---driven by point-estimate $\Delta_{d_T}$ in the wide multi-island inversion CI---so report it with the same CI propagation as $\Delta_{d_T}$ itself. The upper bound is more robust because it does not depend on $\Delta_{d_T}$ when the $d_N$ piece dominates. When the joint $(\phi, \beta)$ inversion CI is empty (the LCA-restricted model is rejected at $\alpha$), the headline cell splits into the identified piece (always reportable: $W_{\text{obs}} - W_{\text{nofm}}$ uses the non-parametric switcher $\Delta_{\underline{d}}$ and the inversion $\Delta_{d_T}$) and the LCA-dependent piece ($W_{\text{opt}} - W_{\text{obs}}$, reportable only when the joint CI is non-empty). This matches the existing CHN-pooled / hukou-split convention.

Within-trajectory heterogeneity in returns

The formula above treats every never-migrant as if they share the same return. Under LCA, $\Delta_i = \beta + \phi\theta_i$ varies within trajectory whenever $\theta_i$ does, so the formula is implicitly a no-within-heterogeneity assumption. The GRC identifies the conditional mean $\Delta_{d_N}$ but not the conditional dispersion. The same caveat applies inside $d_T$ and inside switcher trajectories, but $d_N$ is where the share is biggest and the LCA extrapolation does most of the work.

Direction of error. Optimal sort assigns to urban the workers with $\Delta_i > 0$, so the aggregate gain from $d_N$ is $\pi_{d_N} \cdot E[\max(0, \Delta_i) \mid d_N]$, not $\pi_{d_N} \cdot \max(0, E[\Delta_i \mid d_N])$. With $\Delta_{d_N} > 0$, the conditional expectation of the positive part weakly exceeds the positive part of the conditional mean. The trajectory-mean number is a lower bound on the true gap. Under $\phi<0$, the lowest-$\theta$ never-migrants carry the highest unrealized returns, so optimal sort moves precisely those workers—a feature, not a wrinkle, of the pro-poor finding.

Two reporting options. The floor uses only trajectory means; the envelope adds a parameterized within-$d_N$ dispersion that the reader can dial.

Option 1: conservative floor

Trajectory-mean formula as written: $\sum_{\underline{d}} \pi_{\underline{d}}\,[\max(0, \Delta_{\underline{d}}) - \Delta_{\underline{d}} \bar{D}_{\underline{d}}]$.

Defensible as a lower bound under LCA. No extra structure beyond what the GRC already delivers. This is the back-of-envelope $\approx 22\%$ / $6.5\%$ / $3.7\%$.

How we report it: “at least $X\%$ of consumption is unrealized.”

Option 3: bounded envelope

Parameterize $\theta_i \mid d_N \sim N(\hat\mu_{\theta \mid d_N}, (c\sigma_\theta)^2)$ directly, for $c \in [0, 1]$. At $c = 0$, recovers Option 1 as a point. At $c = 1$, within-$d_N$ dispersion equals the cross-trajectory dispersion of $\mu_{\underline{d}}$, which is itself an upper bound on $\sigma_\theta$ by the law of total variance; $c = 1$ is therefore the upper edge of plausible within-trajectory dispersion.

Integrate $\max(0, \beta + \phi\theta)$ over $\theta \mid d_N$ on a grid of $c$. Plot the gap as a function of $c$; the audience reads the envelope rather than committing to a single value. No decision-rule construction, no $F_\eta$ dependence.

How we report it: a range. The only choice on the reader's table is “how dispersed should you think within-$d_N$ comparative advantage is?”

Auxiliary assumption. The Gaussian $\theta_i \mid d_N$ form inside Option 3 is an auxiliary parametric assumption not implied by the GRC, imported to perform the integration. The Option 1 floor does not require it.

Why no $F_\eta$-based correction. An earlier formulation built $f_{\theta \mid d_N}$ from the model's decision rule (choose urban iff $\beta + \phi\theta_i + \eta_{it} > 0$, $f_{\theta \mid d_N} \propto f_\theta \cdot F_\eta(-\beta - \phi\theta)^T$). Under $\hat\phi < 0$, that rule predicts $\Delta_{d_N} < 0$; the empirical $\hat\Delta_{d_N} > 0$ in all three countries rejects it. A standalone "decision-rule-corrected" magnitude would therefore integrate the wrong density. The envelope above achieves the same goal---bracket what within-trajectory dispersion can do to the gap---without depending on the rejected rule.

The deeper concern: LCA out of the switcher support

$(\beta, \phi)$ are identified from switcher trajectories; both options extrapolate to $d_N$. If LCA is a local approximation around the switcher region, the slope identified from switchers may not carry over to never-migrants, and the $d_N$ piece of every option becomes shaky.

The paper already addresses this through the $J$-test and the inversion CI as the primary inference object. The misallocation reporting inherits the same identification machinery and the same caveats; the options above sit on top of LCA, not next to it.

A first-pass support diagnostic confirms that $\hat\mu_{d_N}$ lies inside the switcher hull for all three countries: CHN at 26% from the lower edge, IDN at 24%, TZA at 8% (boundary case worth flagging in the paper-side figure). Extrapolation is interior, not external, in every case. See the support-diagnostic memo and the per-country density figures.

Open question: what to do near $\phi = -1$

The $\Delta_{d_T}$ piece explodes as $\phi$ approaches $-1$ (Möbius pole; see 2026-04-30 note). For specs where the inversion CI brushes $-1$, report the gap restricted to the $d_N$ and switcher pieces, which captures the headline because $d_T$ is small.

Open also: log points versus level percentage. The log-point version is cleanest given the consumption equation; the level version is cleaner for the abstract but requires a Jensen adjustment.

Cost: Both extremes (no migration vs current, current vs optimal) share a common pipeline---extract trajectory-level $\mu_{\underline{d}}, \Delta_{\underline{d}}, \bar{D}_{\underline{d}}, \pi_{\underline{d}}$ from .ster files and panel data; aggregate; propagate inference through the inversion CI. Pipeline build is 2–3 days; once built, the zero-migration aggregate and Option 1 of the misallocation calc drop out in parallel. Options 2–3 add their own 2–3 days (parametric within-trajectory $\theta_i$ distribution, integration over eq. (6), grid sweep on $c$). Compute cost is small throughout.

2. The hukou puzzle and what removing it would unlock Ready to implement

The CHN hukou-split estimates show inverted patterns across regimes. Rural-hukou never-migrants carry a large unrealized return under a flat LCA slope; urban-hukou never-migrants carry essentially zero return under a steep LCA slope. Read together, the two regimes trace out a sorting-suppressed world and a sorting world side by side.

Mechanical decomposition

Recall that $\Delta_{d_N} = \beta + \phi(\mu_{d_N} - \mu_{\underline{d}_0})$. Two ways for $\Delta_{d_N}$ to come out where it does:

  • Rural-hukou: $\phi \approx 0$ flattens the LCA line, so $\Delta_{d_N} \approx \beta \approx 0.106$. The 10 log point return looks uniform across the comparative-advantage distribution.
  • Urban-hukou: $\phi \approx -1$ tilts the line steeply, and $\beta$ approximately cancels the never-migrant offset $\phi(\mu_{d_N}^{uh} - \mu_{\underline{d}_0}^{uh})$. Never-migrants sit at the point where the line crosses zero.

Economic reading

Rural-hukou: sorting suppressed

The institutional barrier prevents workers from sorting on comparative advantage. The estimated LCA slope is flat not because skills are homogeneous, but because the heterogeneity is not being revealed through migration choices—high-comparative-advantage workers are stuck rural.

The 10-log-point $\Delta_{d_N}^{rh}$ is the unrealized return that sorting would deliver. It looks uniform precisely because the workers who would sort have not been allowed to, so the observed slope cannot distinguish them.

Urban-hukou: sorting works

Workers choose where to locate. Comparative advantage drives the choice. The steep negative $\phi^{uh}$ reflects revealed heterogeneity in returns.

Never-migrants here are not blocked, they are sorted. They chose rural because their personal return to urban location is approximately zero. The $\Delta_{d_N}^{uh} \approx 0$ describes optimally placed workers, not victims of an institutional wedge.

Implication. The hukou system suppresses the comparative-advantage sorting that operates freely for urban-hukou holders. The fact that $\phi$ varies across subsamples is itself the evidence that institutions, not just preferences, are doing real work.

Is this regime-driven, or an LCA misspecification artifact?

A competing reading is that $\Delta_i = \beta + \phi\theta_i$ holds within each regime but with different $(\beta, \phi)$ because the LCA linearity is a local approximation that bends differently when fit to different selected populations. Under that reading, $\phi^{rh} \neq \phi^{uh}$ is a specification fact, not an institutional fact.

The way to discriminate is the hukou-split overid tests. Both $J$-statistics fail to reject in the column-5 specification ($p^{rh} = 0.13$, $p^{uh} = 0.22$). The LCA restriction looks fine within each regime; the institutional reading is the more parsimonious one.

What removing hukou would unlock

The counterfactual question is: what if rural-hukou holders could sort freely? Under the reading above, the high-return workers would migrate, and the residual never-migrants would have expected return close to zero, matching the urban-hukou pattern. The welfare gain is the integral of positive returns over the rural-hukou never-migrant distribution.

$$\text{Welfare gain (lower bound)} \;\approx\; \pi^{rh} \cdot \pi_{d_N}^{rh} \cdot \Delta_{d_N}^{rh} \;\approx\; 0.74 \cdot 0.65 \cdot 0.106 \;\approx\; 5 \text{ log points}$$

The bound is conservative: it assumes the flat $\phi^{rh}$ reflects genuine uniformity of returns, so every rural-hukou never-migrant gets the same 10 log points. If the flat slope is instead a censoring artifact of suppressed sorting, the welfare gain rises above the bound because optimal sort selects the high-return tail.

A maintained-assumption statement before the simulation. The intercept $\beta$ in the GRC absorbs four sources at once: the pecuniary urban premium net of cost-of-living, the cost-of-living difference, compensating differentials for non-pecuniary amenities, and any residual selection not absorbed by $\phi\theta_i$ under LCA misspecification. Setting $\beta^{rh} = \beta^{uh}$ treats the full intercept gap as the institutional barrier---the assumption that the other three sources are equalized across regimes. This is the headline reading (Reading A). A continuum sensitivity (Reading B: sweep the institutional fraction $c \in [0, 1]$ of the intercept gap) lives in an appendix figure for readers who want to dial down the maintained assumption.

Lower bound (now, no simulation)

Existing estimates already pin a $\approx 5$ log point welfare gain from removing hukou. Carry the inversion CI on $\phi^{rh}$ and $\Delta_{d_N}^{rh}$ for a confidence band.

Resorting version (primary)

Use the model's existing decision rule from eq. (6) in [sec_model.tex]. Parameterize the hukou barrier as an additive cost that shifts $\beta^{rh}$ all the way to $\beta^{uh}$; removing hukou sets the wedge to zero. Simulate which rural-hukou workers select urban under the no-barrier rule. Under Reading A, the four-source decomposition of $\beta^{rh} - \beta^{uh}$ attributes the entire gap to the institutional barrier.

$\sigma_\eta$ is reported as a curve over a justified grid (no commitment to a single value), with two curves per country---logistic and normal shocks---so the figure shows both shape and scale dependence.

What simulation adds

Distributional bounds: the lower bound assumes uniform returns; the resorting simulation delivers a magnitude rather than a bound, and a confidence interval that propagates through the choice model.

Headline output

Percentage gain in CHN aggregate consumption from removing the hukou wedge, decomposed into (a) returns realized by the marginal migrant and (b) the unrealized return left in the residual never-migrant pool.

Cost: bound is half a day, sits inside #1's machinery. Resorting version uses the model's existing decision rule and needs (i) a parametric form for the shock distribution $F_\eta$ (reported as a curve under both logistic and normal), (ii) a parameterization of the institutional barrier, (iii) simulation of choice probabilities. 2–3 days once the sorting specification is locked in.

3. From consumption to welfare: valuing the non-pecuniary side Design open

All four counterfactuals above report consumption-side statistics. Converting them to welfare would require putting a value on the non-pecuniary component of $\nu^U_i - \nu^R_i$, which the LCA framework deliberately does not identify on its own. Two routes are available in principle, both with precedent: observable proxies in the Suri (2011) style and a parametric assumption on $\nu$ in the Heckman style. We leave both to future work and sketch them here as the natural extensions, so the consumption-side counterfactuals in #1 and #2 sit on a clear map of what a welfare bridge would add.

Route A: Suri-style observables

Suri (2011, Section 7) resolves her high-counterfactual-returns puzzle by relating predicted $\hat\theta_i$ to observable cost proxies (distance to fertilizer sellers, distance to roads, infrastructure) rather than imputing the cost component from choice probabilities under a distributional assumption. The analogous move for us would be to model the persistent component of $\nu^U_i - \nu^R_i$ as $X_i'\gamma$ for some observable vector $X_i$ that affects non-pecuniary value of location but not the consumption return $\Delta_i$. The decision rule would become “choose urban iff $\beta + \phi\theta_i + X_i'\gamma + (\text{iid component}) > 0$.” The GRC already identifies $\Delta_i$; a probit or logit using $X_i$ alongside the trajectory predictions of $\Delta_i$ would pin down $\gamma$. Welfare counterfactuals would then include $X_i'\gamma$ as the non-pecuniary value the model puts on observed location.

The cross-country payoff of this route would be to generalize the hukou comparison we already have, which only speaks for China. The same exercise could in principle run in Indonesia and Tanzania with appropriate observables. We leave the cross-country extension to future work.

Cross-country observables
  • Distance from birthplace or origin village (direct Suri analog)
  • Family network at origin (relatives in rural area)
  • Ethnic or community ties concentrated by location
  • Marital status and household composition
  • Age and life-cycle position
Country-specific augmentations
  • CHN: hukou (already have); native-province status; family in rural counties
  • IDN: Javanese-or-not; transmigration history; family in origin village
  • TZA: ethnic group; linguistic region; family ties at origin
Exclusion restriction concerns

$X_i$ must affect non-pecuniary value of location but not the consumption return $\Delta_i$. Variables already in the consumption equation (education, age, female) cannot also serve as exclusion restrictions because they enter $\Delta_i$ via $\gamma$. Distance to birthplace and family-network variables are the most defensible candidates.

Headline output

Country-level decomposition: how much of the gap between observed sorting and rational-sorting predictions is attributable to identifiable non-pecuniary observables versus residual unexplained variation. The residual is what the parametric route would then have to value.

Route B: parametric (in parallel)

Maintain the paper's existing A2 (iid $\nu$) and add a parametric form for $\eta_{it} = \nu^U_{it} - \nu^R_{it}$. Two natural choices: type-I extreme value (logit-style choice probabilities, closed-form expected utility, $\sigma_\eta^2 = \pi^2 \sigma^2 / 6$); or normal (probit-style, slightly more numerical work but familiar in empirical micro). Identification: under iid $\eta$,

$$\pi_{d_T} = E_\theta\!\left[(1 - F_\eta(-\Delta(\theta)))^T\right]$$

would pin down $\sigma_\eta$ from the observed trajectory share $\pi_{d_T}$, the GMM-identified distribution of $\Delta_i$, and the panel length $T$. Multiple trajectory shares would give over-identification. Once $\sigma_\eta$ is pinned, the non-pecuniary value to a $d_T$ worker is the truncated mean $E[\eta \mid d_T]$, a standard Heckman-style inverse-Mills-ratio calculation. This is the same machinery Suri uses for her ATE/TT/MTE benchmarks in Section 5.2 of her paper. We leave this implementation to future work.

Why both, if and when we do them. Route A makes the empirical case (“here is what we can attribute to identifiable amenity proxies”). Route B would deliver a single welfare number under a stated distributional assumption (“under iid type-I EV, the welfare gap is X%”). The two are complements, not substitutes. Reporting both would give a defensible range and let the audience choose the reading they trust most. If only one is feasible in a follow-up, logistic and normal as parallel reports under Route B is the cheaper exercise; Route A is the more empirically grounded one. We defer the choice with the welfare bridge itself.

Borjas-style benchmark (later TODO)

Borjas (1987) assumes joint log-normality of $(\theta^U, \theta^R)$ in source and destination, then identifies skill prices and selection patterns from cross-sectional wage moments. Our LCA approach deliberately avoids this distributional assumption. Worth running as a benchmark robustness exercise: estimate $b_U$ and $b_R$ separately under joint log-normality, characterize selection patterns, compare to what LCA delivers.

Panel extensions of Borjas that we should cite or adapt:

  • Heckman & Honoré (1990, ECMA): formal identification result for the Roy model under different data structures including panels.
  • Lemieux (1998, JOLE): panel-based CRC, the direct ancestor of Suri and CKT.
  • Carneiro, Hansen, & Heckman (2003, IER); Cunha, Heckman, & Navarro (2005, 2006); Cunha & Heckman (2007): factor structure approach. Recover the joint distribution of $(\theta^U, \theta^R)$ from panel plus auxiliary measurements without imposing joint normality. The cleanest panel extension of Borjas in spirit.
  • Heckman & Vytlacil (2005, ECMA): MTE framework with panel data; identifies the full distribution of treatment effects.
  • Dahl (2002, ECMA); Kennan & Walker (2011, ECMA): migration-specific. Dahl: multi-destination Roy with selection correction. Kennan-Walker: dynamic Roy of migration with panel, modern structural template.
  • Lagakos, Mobarak, & Waugh (2023): recent migration-specific structural Roy with cross-country data, closest in spirit to our setting.

The natural Borjas-with-panel adaptation for us would be Carneiro-Hansen-Heckman / Cunha-Heckman: factor structure recovery of the joint skill distribution, then Borjas-style selection characterization. Less ambitious than Kennan-Walker; more honest about its parametric structure than vanilla Borjas.

Cost (future work): Route A would require identifying defensible observable proxies and verifying their availability across the three panels (CFPS, IFLS, TZNPS); estimation would then be a probit/logit per country plus integration of $X_i'\gamma$ into the counterfactual aggregates---maybe 3–4 days. Route B would reuse the same trajectory-level estimates and add the $\sigma_\eta$ identification step plus truncated-mean integration---2–3 days. Borjas benchmark is a separate exercise, lower priority.

How to read the results: identification vs behavior

A natural reaction to the negative $\Delta_{d_T}$ and the positive $\Delta_{d_N}$ is: “the results contradict what the model would predict under rational sorting, so why is it OK to estimate this model in the first place?” This section is the defense. The short answer: the parameters are identified by moment conditions that don't require the decision rule to literally describe choice behavior. The model isolates the pecuniary return $\Delta_i$ from non-pecuniary factors; observing that pecuniary returns don't fully explain location choices is a finding the model surfaces, not a failure of the model.

What the moments actually need

The GRC + LCA identification rests on the moment conditions

$$E[\varepsilon_{it} \cdot z_{it}] = 0,$$

where $z_{it}$ contains trajectory indicators and their interactions with $D_{it}$. These moments hold under A1--A5 in sec_model.tex:259--265: time-invariant $\theta_i^l$, i.i.d.\ $\nu^l$, constant $\beta$, symmetric covariate effects ($\gamma^U = \gamma^R$), and LCA. The decision rule in eq. (6) does not enter the moments. It is a narrative device for why trajectories carry information about $\theta_i$. The orthogonality of $\varepsilon$ to trajectory indicators is what does the identification work.

The key distinction. A1–A5 jointly imply the moments hold. Of these, A5 (LCA) is the one formally testable through Hansen's $J$; A1–A4 are maintained. The estimated $(\beta, \phi, \mu_{\underline{d}}, \Delta_{\underline{d}})$ are conditional averages of $\Delta_i$ across trajectories—well-defined population objects. What A2 contributes inside the identification set is the structural reading that trajectory membership cleanly reflects comparative advantage; that reading is what loosens if A2 is strained, while the conditional-average interpretation of the estimates is preserved as long as A5 holds (which $J$ checks).

The $J$-test is the formal check

Hansen's $J$ tests whether the LCA restriction (A5) is consistent with the over-identified moment conditions. If LCA fails badly, $J$ rejects. In our reported specs:

The pooled CHN rejection is exactly why the paper splits by hukou. In every other spec we would report, the data do not reject LCA. The moments hold. The LCA holds. The estimates are reliable as conditional averages.

What the negative $\Delta_{d_T}$ means

It is a finding within the model, not a failure of the model. The model identifies, separately, the pecuniary return $\Delta_i = \beta + \phi\theta_i$ and the non-pecuniary residual $\nu_{it}^l$. The observation that some workers stay urban despite $\Delta_{d_T} < 0$ tells you the non-pecuniary side—$\nu$, or whatever it stands in for—is doing real work for those workers. That is a substantive insight the model enables you to see, not evidence that the model is wrong.

A2 (i.i.d.\ $\nu$) is the piece strained by this finding. The paper already concedes (sec_model.tex:108) that persistent non-pecuniary factors would weaken the trajectory-as-$\theta$ interpretation. A2 plays a specific role in identification: it underwrites the reading that trajectory membership reflects comparative advantage cleanly. The negative $\Delta_{d_T}$ is evidence that A2 is approximately rather than strictly true—workers stay urban for reasons beyond current $\theta$ realizations. The testable consequence is whether A5 (LCA) holds despite A2 being strained, and that is exactly what $J$ checks. In our reported specs $J$ does not reject. The estimates remain consistent estimates of trajectory-conditional averages; what loosens is the structural reading that those averages represent “what rational sorters in trajectory $\underline{d}$ would have realized.”

One real qualification for the counterfactual

Our misallocation gap is a consumption gap, not a welfare gap. If $d_T$ workers stay urban because of persistent non-pecuniary amenity value not captured in $\Delta_i$, the reallocation that puts them rural raises their consumption but lowers their utility. The consumption number is still well-defined and meaningful; it just is not the same as a welfare number. Standard practice in this literature is to report the consumption gap and acknowledge the non-pecuniary caveat. That is the right move here too.

Framing for a referee

The defensible position has four parts.

1. Moment conditions hold

A1–A5 jointly imply the moments. A5 is the testable piece; $J$ does not reject in the specs we report. A2 is maintained, evidently strained by the negative $\Delta_{d_T}$, but not strained enough to violate A5.

2. Negative $\Delta_{d_T}$ is a finding, not a failure

It says non-pecuniary factors are quantitatively important for always-urban workers, consistent with the literature on urban amenities, social ties, and migration costs. The model lets us measure this without making a stand on what those factors are.

3. Consumption side is cleanly identified

The misallocation gap is a consumption-side statistic. A welfare-side statistic would require structure on $\nu$ that the data do not pin down. The consumption number is the conservative, cleanly identified object.

4. Hukou is the validation

Where the institutional barrier is absent (urban-hukou), the LCA estimates align with rational sorting. Where it binds (rural-hukou), they do not. That gives the paper a natural “consumption matters AND institutions matter” reading without overclaiming on the welfare side.

The punchline. The response to “the results are telling you the model is wrong” is: no, the results are using the model to tell us something we couldn't see without it—that non-pecuniary or institutional factors are quantitatively important. The model isolates the pecuniary return; that is its job. The fact that observed migration doesn't perfectly track pecuniary returns is the finding, not the bug.

Sequencing

  1. #1 first. Reuses the inversion CI machinery and delivers the headline number ($\approx 22\%$ TZA, $\approx 6.5\%$ CHN, $\approx 3.7\%$ IDN).
  2. #2 bound, in parallel. The $\approx 5$ log point lower bound on the welfare cost of hukou falls out of #1's machinery applied to the rural-hukou subsample; no new model.
  3. #2 resort next. Uses the model's existing decision rule (eq.~6) with $\sigma_\eta$ reported as a curve over a justified grid and both shock distributions (logistic and normal) shown in parallel; converts the bound into a magnitude-plus-CI and tells the cleaner policy story.
  4. #3 deferred to future work. Route A (Suri-style observables) would require cataloging available proxies across CFPS, IFLS, TZNPS and re-estimating with the choice equation augmented; Route B (parametric) would reuse #1's pipeline plus a $\sigma_\eta$ identification step. We leave both to a follow-up.

#1 and #2 (bound) are paper-ready additions in their current scope. #2 (resort) is a model extension that ships in this round. #3 is the route from consumption-side to welfare-side counterfactuals, deferred to future work. A Borjas-style benchmark with panel extensions sits in the back of our mind as a robustness exercise for later (see the expert pocket under #3).
