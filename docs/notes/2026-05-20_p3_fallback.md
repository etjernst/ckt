## What P3 does

When the joint $(\phi, \beta)$ CI crosses the identification boundary at $\phi = -1$, the always-urban formula $\Delta_{d_T} = (\beta + \phi(\alpha_{d_T}^{\text{obs}} - \mu_{\text{base}}))/(1+\phi)$ inflates from the singularity in the denominator.
The aggregate's convex hull then has an enormous upper bound that is a function of the singularity rather than honest uncertainty about the always-urban return.

P3 fallback: at each accepted lattice point, set $\Delta_{d_T} = 0$ and re-evaluate the aggregate.
The population shares $\pi_d$ are unchanged.
This treats the always-urban subpopulation as contributing nothing to either the value-of-observed-migration or the misallocation gap.
Since the misallocation gap is non-negative per trajectory, zeroing out $\Delta_{d_T}$ gives a defensible lower bound on the true gap.
The value-of-observed-migration loses the $\pi_{d_T} \Delta_{d_T} \bar D_{d_T}$ term (which equals $\pi_{d_T} \Delta_{d_T}$ since $\bar D_{d_T} = 1$); this is the contribution attributable to always-urban workers being in the urban location.

Both versions (with and without $d_T$) are reported in the driver output for transparency.

## Results across the four cells

### IDN col 5

| object | with $d_T$ | P3 (no $d_T$) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Value of observed migration | $[-23.57\%, +48.74\%]$ | $+5.14\%$ (point) |
| Misallocation gap | $[+5.67\%, +45.59\%]$ | $[+5.67\%, +6.14\%]$ |

The with-$d_T$ upper bound of $+45.59\%$ on the misallocation gap was driven by the lattice crossing the identification boundary.
P3 narrows the gap to about $5.7\%$--$6.1\%$, a defensible reading.

### TZA col 5

| object | with $d_T$ | P3 (no $d_T$) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Value of observed migration | $[-54.66\%, +154.30\%]$ | $+4.36\%$ (point) |
| Misallocation gap | $[+14.34\%, +171.00\%]$ | $[+14.34\%, +22.67\%]$ |

TZA's accepted region runs from $\phi = -1.37$ to $\phi = -0.40$, straddling the boundary.
The with-$d_T$ upper bound of $+171\%$ on the gap was pole-driven; P3 reduces it to $+22.67\%$, in line with the kind of magnitude the prior literature reports.

### CHN-RF (rural-hukou-first) col 5

| object | with $d_T$ | P3 (no $d_T$) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Value of observed migration | $[+5.36\%, +6.77\%]$ | $+3.95\%$ (point) |
| Misallocation gap | $[+10.49\%, +11.69\%]$ | $[+10.49\%, +11.69\%]$ |

RF's accepted region $[-0.300, -0.070]$ sits well above the boundary, so the with-$d_T$ aggregate is honest; the P3 version differs only in the value-of-migration, where the $d_T$ piece contributes about $+1.4\%$ to $+2.8\%$ (the always-urban consumption advantage).
The misallocation gap is unchanged because the with-$d_T$ gap was already well-behaved.

### CHN-UF (urban-hukou-first) col 5

| object | with $d_T$ | P3 (no $d_T$) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Value of observed migration | $[-99.92\%, +138{,}727\%]$ | $+5.25\%$ (point) |
| Misallocation gap | $[+0.91\%, +130{,}660\%]$ | $[+0.91\%, +1.25\%]$ |

UF's marginal $\phi$ projection $[-3.50, -0.71]$ crosses the boundary, so the with-$d_T$ aggregate is unusable.
P3 yields a defensible UF misallocation gap of about $0.9\%$--$1.3\%$, an order of magnitude smaller than RF's $10.5\%$--$11.7\%$.
Reading: among urban-hukou-first workers, sorting is close to optimal because the institutional barrier does not bind in this regime; the residual gap is small.

## Cross-cell pattern

| cell | gap (P3) | reading |
|---|---:|---|
| IDN | $[+5.7\%, +6.1\%]$ | small, lumped-switcher dominates |
| TZA | $[+14.3\%, +22.7\%]$ | large, $d_N$ dominates |
| CHN-RF | $[+10.5\%, +11.7\%]$ | large, $d_N$ dominates |
| CHN-UF | $[+0.9\%, +1.3\%]$ | small, regime-specific (barrier doesn't bind) |

The RF-vs-UF contrast is the headline E2 finding the paper draft anticipates: when the institutional barrier binds (RF, rural-hukou), substantial consumption is left on the table because high-return workers cannot sort; when the barrier does not bind (UF, urban-hukou), workers are sorted close to optimum and the residual misallocation is small.
