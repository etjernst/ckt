\section*{Reviewer 1's Comments}
%==============================================================================
This comment is very informative about the identification challenge in CRC models. My
suggestions below are mainly about how this comment relates to the literature.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Comparison with the discussion of identification in \citet{S2011}.
    \begin{itemize}
      \item The comment seems to suggests that \citet{S2011} did not discuss the identification challenges in CRC models. However, it should be noted that \citet{S2011} did recognize and discuss these challenges towards the end of section 4.5.1. Empirical Identification of the CRC Model. Specifically, \citet{S2011} acknowledged that $\phi$ can be identified based on two different ratios of reduced-form parameters. 
      \begin{quote}
        We apologize that our comment came across as implying that \citet{S2011} did not consider these issues. 
        This was certainly not our intention.
        We respond to the three points in this section below.
    \end{quote}
      \item Furthermore, \citet{S2011} informally assessed whether the denominator in the identification formula is bounded away from zero. While the authors' reduced-form approach provides valuable insights, it is important to give credit to \citet{S2011} for addressing the identification challenges and considering the identification strength, even if they chose to pursue a minimum distance approach.
      \item Therefore, I recommend that the manuscript provides a fairer representation of Suri (2011)'s contributions in this regard.
      \begin{quote}
        We have revised our comment to highlight the discussion in \citet{S2011} and the paper's acknowledgement of the possibility for weak identification in the following footnote in the introduction:
        
        \textit{It is worth noting that \citet{S2011} discusses ways of estimating $\phi$ as a combination of production function reduced-form parameters and recognizes that identification of $\phi$ relies on a specific restriction on these parameters. Specifically, see the discussion in Section 4.5.1 in \citet{S2011}, where the author also informally assesses whether the denominator in these reduced-form expressions is bounded away from zero.}

        We also tone down the language suggesting that we ``uncover'' the potential for weak identification in the first sentence of that paragraph:
        
        \textit{We investigate these discrepancies and relate them to the potential for weak identification of $\phi$.}
          
      \end{quote}
    \end{itemize}
    
  \item Comparison with the estimation strategy of \citet{V2020}
  \begin{itemize}
      \item The authors might want to cite a recent paper by \citet{V2020} , who also proposed a similar approach to extrapolate from ``movers'' to ``stayers'' using correlated random coefficient models. I think it would be helpful if the authors compare their approach since \citet{V2020} also proposed an estimator based on reduced-form estimates. Suri (2011) is also the empirical example of \citet{V2020}. In my view, the authors made a clear contribution by proposing the Anderson-Rubin confidence interval.
  \end{itemize}
\begin{quote}
    First of all, we would like to clarify that the omission of \citet{V2020}'s paper was unintentional. We had cited it in a previous draft but the discussion and citation got dropped as we attempted to shorten the paper into the current comment. We apologize for this oversight.\newline 
    
    Following your advice, and requests by the editor, we have added the following footnote in the introduction right after we mention the linearity-in-comparative-advantage restriction:\\
    
    \emph{\citet{V2020} shows that the approach of \citet{S2011} relies on a linear extrapolation that is valid if the factors that determine selection other than the treatment effect are uncorrelated with outcomes, and proposes a more robust extrapolation approach that allows for selection into treatment to be correlated with treatment effects as well as (a subset of) covariates. }
\end{quote}
\end{enumerate}
