\subsection{Pooling balanced and unbalanced observers}
\label{sec:unbalanced-proposition}

The estimation sample is unbalanced: in country $c$ with wave count
$T_c \in\{3,4,5\}$, between $88.6\%$ and $95.7\%$ of individuals are
observed in strictly fewer than $T_c$ waves. Following
Section~\ref{sec:model}, a trajectory label $\underline d_i\in\mathcal{D}$
is defined only for individuals observed in all $T_c$ waves. Unbalanced
observers are not assigned a trajectory. Rather than discarding them,
we pool the unbalanced observers into the estimating equation through
a single indicator $U_i\equiv\mathbf{1}\{|\mathcal{T}_i|<T_c\}$,
interacted with the urban dummy:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:restricted-grc-unbalanced}
\begin{aligned}
y_{it} = \; &
\sum_{\underline d \in \mathcal{D}} \mu_{\underline d}\,
\mathbf{1}\{\underline d_i=\underline d\}
+
\sum_{\underline d \in \mathcal{D}_S} \Delta_{\underline d}\,
\mathbf{1}\{\underline d_i=\underline d\}\, D_{it} \\
& +\, \alpha\, U_i
+ \pi\, U_i\, D_{it}
+ x_{it}'\gamma
+ \varepsilon_{it},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with the linear comparative advantage restriction
$\Delta_{\underline d}=\beta+\phi\,E[\theta_i\mid\underline
d_i=\underline d]$ imposed on the switcher coefficients as in
equation~\eqref{eq:restricted-GRC}. Because $\underline d_i$ is
undefined when $U_i=1$, the indicators $\{\mathbf{1}\{\underline
d_i=\underline d\}\}_{\underline d\in\mathcal{D}}$ are identically zero
on the unbalanced subsample. The pair $(\alpha, \pi)$ absorbs the
level and the average urban return of the unbalanced stratum through a
single pooled cell.\footnote{%
In the Stata implementation this corresponds to the coding
$\texttt{trajectory}=999$ for all unbalanced observers, the
$\texttt{i.trajectory}$ factor expansion in the regression, and the
$\texttt{i.unbalanced\#i.choice}$ interaction in
$\texttt{5\_GrRC.do}$ and the corresponding GMM moment system in
$\texttt{run\_grc}$.}

We now show that the estimator of $\{\Delta_{\underline
d}\}_{\underline d\in\mathcal{D}_S}$ delivered by
\eqref{eq:restricted-grc-unbalanced} is consistent for the target
parameters of Section~\ref{sec:model}, and that the inclusion of
unbalanced observers weakly reduces asymptotic variance relative to
dropping them.

\begin{assumption}[Missing at random, conditional on covariates]
\label{ass:mar}
$U_i \perp (\theta_i, \tau_i, \{\nu_{it}^l\}_{t,l})\mid x_{i1},\dots,x_{iT_c}$.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{ass:mar} requires that non-response depends at most
on observed covariates. We view this as defensible in the CFPS, IFLS,
and TZNPS samples given the rich demographic controls, and probe it
directly by comparing balanced- and unbalanced-panel estimates in
Tables~\ref{tab:GRC_CHN_consumption_urban_bal},
\ref{tab:GRC_IDN_consumption_urban_bal}, and
\ref{tab:GRC_TZA_consumption_urban_bal}.

\begin{proposition}[Pooling balanced and unbalanced observers]
\label{prop:pooling}
Under Assumptions A1--A5 of Section~\ref{sec:model} and
Assumption~\ref{ass:mar}, the two-step GMM estimator of
\eqref{eq:restricted-grc-unbalanced} is consistent and asymptotically
normal for $(\beta,\phi,\gamma)$ and for the collection of
balanced-panel trajectory parameters $\{(\mu_{\underline d},
\Delta_{\underline d})\}_{\underline d\in\mathcal{D}}$.
Its asymptotic variance is weakly smaller than that of the
corresponding estimator restricted to balanced observers only.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The argument has two steps: an orthogonality step that isolates the
contribution of balanced observers to
$\{(\mu_{\underline d},\Delta_{\underline d})\}$, and a consistency
step for the covariate and nuisance parameters.

\emph{Step 1 (orthogonality).}
By construction, $\mathbf{1}\{\underline d_i=\underline d\}=0$ for
every $\underline d\in\mathcal{D}$ whenever $U_i=1$, because the
trajectory label is only defined on the balanced subsample. Hence for
every $\underline d\in\mathcal{D}$ and every wave $t$,
\begin{equation*}
\mathbf{1}\{\underline d_i=\underline d\}\cdot U_i = 0,
\qquad
\mathbf{1}\{\underline d_i=\underline d\}\cdot U_i D_{it} = 0.
\end{equation*}
The design matrix therefore block-separates: the balanced regressors
$W_1 \equiv \bigl(\mathbf{1}\{\underline d_i=\underline d\},\,
\mathbf{1}\{\underline d_i=\underline d\}\, D_{it}\bigr)_{\underline
d\in\mathcal{D}}$ are orthogonal in the sample to the unbalanced
controls $W_2\equiv(U_i,\, U_i D_{it})$. By the
Frisch--Waugh--Lovell theorem, the GMM estimator of
$(\mu,\Delta)$ is numerically equal to the estimator obtained by
first partialling out $W_2$ and $x_{it}$, then fitting the residuals
to the residualised $W_1$ using only the balanced subsample (since
$W_1$ is zero elsewhere).

\emph{Step 2 (consistency of the partialling-out).}
Step~1 requires consistent estimation of $(\gamma,\alpha,\pi)$ from
the pooled sample. Under A1 and A2, the conditional mean of $y_{it}$
given $(\underline d_i,D_{it},U_i,x_{it})$ separates additively as in
\eqref{eq:restricted-grc-unbalanced}. Under Assumption~\ref{ass:mar},
$U_i$ carries no information about $(\theta_i,\tau_i,\nu)$ beyond
what $x_{it}$ already contains, so
\begin{equation*}
E[\varepsilon_{it}\mid\underline d_i, D_{it}, U_i, x_{it}]
= 0
\end{equation*}
on both the balanced stratum (where $\underline d_i$ is observed) and
the unbalanced stratum (where $\underline d_i$ is missing and the
conditioning reduces to $D_{it}, U_i, x_{it}$). Standard GMM
arguments (Hansen, 1982) deliver $\sqrt{n}$-consistency and
asymptotic normality of the full parameter vector in
\eqref{eq:restricted-grc-unbalanced}, including the balanced
$(\mu_{\underline d},\Delta_{\underline d})$.

\emph{Step 3 (efficiency).}
Let $\hat\gamma^{\mathrm{pool}}$ denote the covariate coefficient
estimated on the full sample and $\hat\gamma^{\mathrm{bal}}$ the
analogous estimator on balanced observers only. Under
Assumption~\ref{ass:mar}, both are consistent for $\gamma$, and
$\hat\gamma^{\mathrm{pool}}$ has weakly smaller asymptotic variance
because it uses strictly more observations. The partialling-out
residual $M_{W_2,x}y$ that enters Step~1 therefore has weakly smaller
variance under pooling, which carries over to
$(\mu_{\underline d},\Delta_{\underline d})$ via the sandwich variance
formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The orthogonality of Step~1 is the substantive content of the
proposition. Because the trajectory indicators are identically zero
for unbalanced observers, the unbalanced subsample does not
contribute directly to the identification of any $\Delta_{\underline
d}$ of economic interest; it enters
\eqref{eq:restricted-grc-unbalanced} only through the estimation of
$\gamma$, period shifters, and the pooled pair $(\alpha,\pi)$. As a
result, the balanced-panel estimates are robust to substantially
stronger forms of mis-specification for the unbalanced stratum than
Assumption~\ref{ass:mar} requires. Even if attrition were
non-ignorable on the unbalanced subsample, the bias would operate
only through $\hat\gamma$, and the block structure of the score
implies that the induced bias in $\hat\Delta_{\underline d}$ is of
smaller order whenever
$\hat\gamma^{\mathrm{pool}}\overset{p}{\to}\gamma$.%
\footnote{Formally, if $\hat\gamma^{\mathrm{pool}}\to\gamma^\ast\ne\gamma$
  with bias $b=\gamma^\ast-\gamma$, then the induced bias in
  $\hat\Delta_{\underline d}$ equals $-(W_1'M_{W_2}W_1)^{-1}W_1'M_{W_2}Xb$,
  which vanishes when $W_1$ is orthogonal to $X$ after residualising
  on $W_2$. In our specification this is approximately satisfied
  because trajectory cells pool across covariate values within the
  balanced stratum.}
The pooled specification is therefore conservative: it never loses
information relative to the balanced-only estimator and is weakly
more efficient whenever $\gamma$ is correctly specified in both
strata.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
An immediate corollary is that the empirical gap between the balanced-only
and pooled estimates of $\Delta_{\underline d}$, reported in
Tables~\ref{tab:GRC_CHN_consumption_urban_bal},
\ref{tab:GRC_IDN_consumption_urban_bal}, and
\ref{tab:GRC_TZA_consumption_urban_bal}, provides a direct empirical
check on Assumption~\ref{ass:mar}: systematic divergence would signal
either mis-specified covariate effects across strata or a violation of
MAR that contaminates $\hat\gamma^{\mathrm{pool}}$. The close
correspondence we observe is consistent with both conditions holding
in the CFPS, IFLS, and TZNPS panels.
\end{remark}
